## Interview guide

Keep each conversation to 25–40 minutes. Do not explain the product until the final three minutes, if at all.

Ask:

- “Tell me about the last time `{trigger}` happened.”
- “What led up to it?”
- “What did you do next?”
- “Who else was involved?”
- “How often has this happened recently?”
- “What did it cost in time, money, risk, or lost business?”
- “How do you handle it today?”
- “Which tools, people, spreadsheets, or workarounds are involved?”
- “What have you already tried and abandoned?”
- “What did those approaches cost?”
- “Who owns the budget or decides whether the workflow changes?”
- “Tell me about the last time you switched tools or processes.”
- “Why did you switch, and what made it difficult?”
- “What happens if this remains unchanged for another year?”
- “Who else should I speak with?”

Avoid:

- “Would you use my product?”
- “Is this a good idea?”
- “Would you pay for it?”
- “How much would you pay?”
- Leading descriptions of how painful the problem ought to be.

Record concrete incidents and behavior, not compliments.

## What to capture after every interview

For each conversation, record:

- Interview number and date.
- Participant role and relevant context.
- Cold or warm recruitment source.
- Most recent incident and approximate timing.
- Frequency of the problem.
- Named tools and workarounds.
- Existing time or money cost.
- Previously abandoned solutions.
- Past switching behavior and barriers.
- Budget owner.
- Short verbatim quotations.
- Whether the interview supports, contradicts, or is neutral on each gate.
- Any leading questions, pitching, or future-tense questions you accidentally used.
- Pitch leakage score from 0–5, where lower is better.

## Decision gates

After ten genuine interviews, segment participants into:

- **Bleeding and paying:** frequent concrete pain plus existing expenditure of money, labor, or risk.
- **Bleeding, not paying:** real pain but little action or cost.
- **Polite only:** opinions, compliments, and hypothetical interest without relevant behavior.

Score each gate from 1–5:

- **G1 Pain:** repeated, recent incidents with meaningful frequency and consequences.
- **G2 Spend:** existing money, labor, workarounds, or risk committed to solving it.
- **G3 Switch:** evidence of active searching, previous switching, accessible budget, and manageable migration barriers.

Use these decision rules:

- **Advance to a smoke test or very thin MVP:** repeated independent behavioral evidence supports all three gates, with a clearly identifiable bleeding-and-paying segment. Do not interpret this as permission for a large build.
- **Reshape:** pain is repeated, but spending or switching evidence points to a different customer, trigger, or narrower wedge.
- **Park:** pain is rare or mild, interviewees take no meaningful action, or switching is structurally implausible.

A practical success signal is at least four or five independent target customers describing closely related recent incidents, with several already paying a concrete cost and showing credible switching behavior. It is pattern evidence, not market sizing.
