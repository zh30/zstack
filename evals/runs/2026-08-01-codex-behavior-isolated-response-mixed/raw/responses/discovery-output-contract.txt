## 7. Decision

**Verdict: Advance**

Advance only to a bounded concierge pilot and smoke test—not a broad product build.

The evidence supports this hypothesis:

> Service-business operators with recurring, cross-tool client onboarding will test a low-migration workflow that reduces manual transfer while preserving ownership, CSV portability, and control of sensitive files.

Initial ICP:

- Agency operations or project managers with recurring client onboarding
- Bookkeeping or similar service teams using paid administrative labor
- Must have a recent onboarding failure or at least 30 minutes of manual transfer per onboarding
- Must already use multiple tools or paid human assistance

Initial exclusions:

- Founders or teams without a recent onboarding problem
- Prospects offering only praise or hypothetical interest
- Workflows requiring sensitive-file ingestion before security controls are established
- Full-stack migrations during initial validation

## 8. Concrete downstream handoff

Run a concierge pilot before writing a full PRD:

- **Owner:** Founder
- **Recruitment:** Convert S01’s request into a scheduled pilot, then recruit two additional cold participants matching the narrow ICP.
- **Workflow:** Take one real onboarding from intake through tool handoff while preserving the participant’s current systems.
- **Pilot scope:** Structured intake, field mapping, explicit owner per task, CSV import/export, and a manual-review fallback.
- **Do not build yet:** General workflow builder, native integrations marketplace, analytics suite, or file repository.
- **Bookkeeper condition:** Use redacted or synthetic files until access, storage, retention, and deletion requirements are agreed.
- **Baseline:** Record current elapsed time, hands-on time, tool count, failures, rework, and paid labor.
- **Success:** At least 2 of 3 participants complete a real onboarding; each reduces hands-on transfer time by at least 50%; no critical ownership or data-handling failure occurs; and at least 1 commits money or a second live onboarding.
- **Fail/reshape:** Fewer than 2 complete, migration/setup consumes most of the saved time, sensitive-data handling blocks use, or nobody commits to continued use.
- **After success:** Send only the validated workflow and constraints to `z-write-prd`; use the observed before/after behavior for `z-landing-smoke`.

Copy concepts for a smoke test—paraphrases, not interview quotes:

1. Reduce repeated copying between onboarding tools.
2. Keep every onboarding task visibly owned.
3. Work with the current stack without a full migration.
4. Preserve CSV import and export.
5. Keep sensitive client files under explicit control.

Open questions for the next interviews:

- What happened in the most recent onboarding failure?
- How often does the workflow occur?
- Which fields are copied, between which tools, and by whom?
- What does the existing workaround cost per onboarding?
- Who owns the budget and approves workflow changes?
- What data may leave the current systems?
- What made previous migration or template adoption fail?
- What concrete event would cause them to abandon the new workflow?
