## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem in user language:** Support agents repeatedly rebuild the browser context needed to work on a particular client, losing time at the beginning of a shift or when switching clients.
- **Proposed solution:** A Chrome extension that saves and restores named, client-specific tab workspaces.
- **Narrow ICP:** Chrome-based customer-support teams of roughly 5–20 agents, serving recurring named clients, with frequent client switching and enough local autonomy to install an extension.
- **Likely buyer:** Support lead or support-operations manager.
- **Initial end user:** An agent repeatedly opening the same client-specific tools, dashboards, tickets, documents, and admin pages.
- **Non-ICP:** Large enterprises with centrally blocked extensions; single-client agents; teams already satisfied with bookmarks, profiles, or saved tab groups; workflows involving highly sensitive page capture.
- **Success signal:** A support lead commits money or a deposit for a pilot after seeing a minimal restore workflow.
- **Kill signal:** Target agents cannot show recurring reconstruction in their recent work, native Chrome features solve it adequately, administrators prevent installation, or support leads decline even a small paid pilot.

### 3.4 Pricing and willingness-to-pay

A dated, unverified capture places an adjacent paid tool around **$7–12 per user per month**. This is only a pricing hypothesis; it does not show that support teams will pay the same amount for this outcome.

At that range, the $500 MRR goal requires:

- At **$12/seat/month:** `ceil(500 ÷ 12) = 42 seats`
- At **$10/seat/month:** `500 ÷ 10 = 50 seats`
- At **$7/seat/month:** `ceil(500 ÷ 7) = 72 seats`

A representative route would be five 10-seat teams at $10 per seat, but neither the team size nor conversion rate is validated. The immediate commercial test should therefore be a paid team pilot, not a free-user count.

## 6. Commercial value

**Verdict: Mixed.**

The economic buyer is probably a support lead paying to reduce shift setup, client-switching, and handoff friction across multiple agents. Daily recurrence and an estimated 10–20 minutes lost per shift make the outcome potentially valuable. Team workspaces could also move the product from personal convenience into operational consistency.

The commercial case remains incomplete because the time loss has not been measured, the affected seat count is unknown, and no buyer has paid. Chrome’s native features and bookmarks may already be “good enough.” The proposed product must sell a measurable support-operations outcome rather than tab organization.

## 7. Independent-developer fit

**Verdict: Conditional.**

A four-week MVP is feasible if it remains deliberately small:

- Save URLs and tab order into named client workspaces.
- Restore a workspace with one click.
- Keep data local by default.
- Share a workspace as a simple exported template or controlled link.
- Avoid capturing page contents, credentials, cookies, or application state.

The dangerous scope is real-time team synchronization, permissions, analytics, identity management, enterprise deployment, and broad browser compatibility. Those features increase security scrutiny and support burden before payment is established.

The $500 budget is sufficient for a prototype and direct validation, assuming the founder can build a Chrome extension. It is not sufficient to solve enterprise compliance or buy meaningful broad distribution. Initial sales should therefore be founder-led to the known community and three support leads.

## 8. Short-term return: 30–90 days

**Verdict: Low odds of reaching $500 MRR; plausible odds of obtaining an initial paid pilot.**

- **Success metric:** $500 in recurring monthly commitments, not waitlist signups or free installs.
- **Best plausible path:** Sell a lightweight team pilot directly to small support teams, then expand seats inside successful accounts.
- **Why odds are low:** The target requires 42–72 paid seats at the only available price reference; there is no verified payment signal, distribution channel, or evidence that teams will replace bookmarks/native Chrome workflows.
- **What would falsify the path:** Daily-pain participants cannot demonstrate the workflow; support leads will not commit to a paid pilot; administrators reject the minimal permission model; or the prototype fails to save meaningful time compared with saved groups or bookmarks.

A reasonable 14-day continuation gate is **two paid team pilots or deposits**, plus demonstrated repeated use by agents. Interest without a financial commitment should not justify building team infrastructure.

## 9. Entry directions

### Primary wedge: client launchpads for small multi-client support teams

- **Who:** Teams of 5–20 Chrome-based agents handling several recurring client accounts, without centrally blocked extensions.
- **Job to be done:** “When I start a shift, switch clients, or take over from another agent, restore the correct client tools immediately without reconstructing them from memory.”
- **MVP:** Named URL/tab bundles, one-click restore, local storage, and a simple manager-created template that agents can import. No live synchronization initially.
- **Differentiation:** Repeatable client handoff and manager-defined context, rather than generic tab saving.
- **First users:** The five reported daily sufferers and teams represented by the three support leads.
- **14-day metric:** Two paid pilots or deposits, with agents restoring workspaces on at least three separate working days and reporting a clearly better result than their existing workaround.

### Backup wedges

- **Agency account switching:** Small agencies repeatedly moving among client dashboards, documents, and admin portals.
- **Personal workflow utility:** A local-only individual extension, but only if team buyers reject deployment while individuals show payment intent.

### Explicitly do not start with

- A generic tab manager for all Chrome users.
- Enterprise-wide deployment or administrative controls.
- Real-time collaboration, role-based access, analytics, or cross-browser support.
- Capturing cookies, credentials, page contents, or authenticated session state.
- Building all four weeks before asking for payment.

## 10. Risks and open questions

- Whether the reported pain reflects recent behavior rather than hypothetical preference.
- Whether 10–20 minutes per shift can be measured objectively.
- Whether the problem occurs at shift start, during client switching, during handoffs, or all three.
- Whether saved groups, profiles, or bookmark folders solve most cases after minor process changes.
- Whether support leads own a software budget and can approve extensions.
- Which Chrome permissions administrators will accept.
- Whether client workspace definitions contain sensitive URLs or customer identifiers.
- Whether agents need fixed templates or personal variations.
- Whether the two team-workspace requests represent buying organizations.
- Whether an adjacent tool’s price is current, comparable, or commercially successful.
- Whether direct access to the noted community is sufficient to reach 42–72 seats in 90 days.
