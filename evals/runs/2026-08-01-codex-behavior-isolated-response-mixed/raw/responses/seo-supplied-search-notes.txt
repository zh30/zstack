## 1. Executive decision

**Primary cluster:** `client onboarding workflow for agencies`

**Primary keyword:** `client onboarding workflow for agencies`

**Primary page:** Homepage `/`

**Positioning wedge:** Help small agencies make every onboarding step explicitly owned, visible, and actionable—not merely documented in a checklist.

This is the best initial cluster because it combines the product category, the agency audience, and the workflow problem. The supplied notes suggest that generic software queries are dominated by established CRM and portal vendors, while agency-workflow and owner-specific results are more mixed. That makes a focused workflow page more plausible for a new domain than a broad “client onboarding software” campaign.

Demand and difficulty remain **unknown**, not quantified. Autocomplete-style phrases, mixed-result notes, and community wording are preliminary search signals only because they could not be independently verified.

## 2. Audience and search context

| Searcher | Job to be done | Search mindset |
|---|---|---|
| Small-agency founder | Stop onboarding work from falling through gaps | “I need a process my team will actually follow.” |
| Account or project lead | Know who owns each next step | “Who is responsible, and what is blocked?” |
| Operations lead | Standardize onboarding across clients | “I need a reusable workflow or template.” |

The primary problem statement should be:

> Client onboarding breaks when a checklist names the work but not the owner, handoff, due date, or next action.

Product claims must remain limited to capabilities that actually exist. Do not claim improved completion rates or faster onboarding without product data.

## 6. Homepage brief

**Suggested title:**  
`Client Onboarding Workflow for Small Agencies`

**H1:**  
`A client onboarding workflow where every next step has an owner`

**Meta description:**  
`Run repeatable client onboarding without dropped handoffs. Assign every step, clarify what happens next, and keep your small agency moving.`

**Canonical URL:**  
`https://{domain}/`

### Recommended structure

1. **Hero**
   - State the agency audience.
   - State the ownership problem.
   - Show one primary CTA.
   - Avoid generic claims such as “streamline your business.”

2. **Why onboarding checklists stall**
   - A task exists but nobody owns it.
   - Client and agency responsibilities are unclear.
   - Handoffs do not identify the next action.
   - Status updates depend on manual follow-up.

3. **How the workflow works**
   - Start from a repeatable process.
   - Assign one accountable owner per step.
   - Define the next action and due condition.
   - Expose blocked or waiting work.
   - Reuse the workflow for the next client.

4. **Example workflow**
   - Contract completed.
   - Client information requested.
   - Access received.
   - Kickoff prepared.
   - Kickoff held.
   - Delivery handoff completed.

   Each example should show owner, status, and next action—not just task names.

5. **Agency-specific use cases**
   - Marketing agencies
   - Design or development studios
   - Consultants with repeatable onboarding

   Include these only if the product genuinely supports their processes.

6. **Template entry point**
   - Link to the workflow-template page using descriptive anchor text such as “client onboarding workflow template.”

7. **Automation**
   - Describe actual triggers, reminders, assignments, or integrations.
   - If automation is not yet implemented, omit this section rather than describing a roadmap as a current feature.

8. **FAQ**
   - What is a client onboarding workflow?
   - Who should own client onboarding?
   - What is the difference between a checklist and a workflow?
   - How do small agencies prevent dropped handoffs?
   - Can the workflow be reused for every client?

9. **Final CTA**
   - Use the same conversion action as the hero.

### Homepage proof requirements

Add product screenshots or a short annotated workflow example. Show the ownership mechanism directly. Avoid invented testimonials, customer counts, time savings, or completion-rate claims.

## 8. Technical baseline

Because the site and code could not be inspected, each status starts as “verify.”

| Item | Required action |
|---|---|
| Indexability | Confirm all four public pages return `200`, render useful HTML without authentication, and lack accidental `noindex` directives |
| Canonicals | Add one self-referencing canonical per indexable page; normalize protocol, host, trailing slashes, and parameters |
| Robots | Provide a valid `robots.txt`; do not block page assets needed for rendering |

| Metadata | Give every page a unique title, H1, and description aligned with its assigned intent |

## 9. Measurement plan

The first objective is to establish a baseline, not hit an invented traffic target.

| Signal | Method | Decision use |
|---|---|---|
| Indexed pages | GSC Page Indexing and URL Inspection | Diagnose discovery or technical failures |
| Non-brand impressions | GSC query filter excluding brand | Determine whether the cluster earns visibility |
| Query-to-page alignment | GSC query and page reports | Detect cannibalization and mismatched intent |
| Clicks and CTR | GSC by page/query | Improve titles only after impressions exist |
| Organic CTA actions | Analytics events with landing-page attribution | Identify pages that assist conversion |
| Signup/waitlist completions | Analytics plus product records where practical | Evaluate commercial relevance |
| Template interactions | Copy/download/use event | Test whether template intent produces action |
| Backlinks/referrals | GSC Links and analytics referrals | Track earned distribution without paid tools |
| Manual SERP sample | Monthly US-localized, logged consistently | Observe result type and page movement; not a volume estimate |

### Instrumentation events

Use stable names such as:

- `primary_cta_clicked`
- `signup_started`
- `signup_completed` or `waitlist_joined`
- `template_viewed`
- `template_used`
- `pricing_viewed`

Record landing page and first-touch source where privacy and implementation permit.

### Decision gates

**Day 30:** Confirm indexing, query tracking, and conversion events. No demand conclusion should be drawn if pages have only recently been indexed.

**Day 60:** Check whether impressions and queries align with agency-workflow, checklist, template, or ownership language. Rewrite mismatched snippets and copy; do not add more pages merely because traffic is low.

**Day 90:**

- Continue the cluster if relevant non-brand visibility is emerging or organic visitors are taking meaningful product actions.
- Narrow toward the query family showing the clearest relevance.
- Consolidate pages if query overlap appears.
- Reassess the cluster before further publishing if there is no relevant visibility after technical discovery and indexing have been verified.

These are evidence-based gates, not promised performance thresholds.

9. Draft the workflow-template page outline and gather a real product example.

**Effort ceiling:** Approximately 24–26 hours across the first month.

### Days 31–60: publish the intent bridge

**Outcome:** Searchers can move from a practical template to the ownership-aware product.

1. Publish `/client-onboarding-workflow-template/`.
2. Include a concrete reusable workflow with owners, handoffs, and completion conditions.
3. Link the template to the homepage, checklist, and pricing.
4. Add the template to the sitemap and request indexing where appropriate.
5. Review GSC queries and page assignments after sufficient collection time.
6. Adjust titles or headings only where actual query evidence reveals an intent mismatch.
7. Improve screenshots and product proof based on visitor behavior or founder/customer feedback.
8. Seek a small number of editorially relevant mentions through founder relationships, agency communities, template roundups, or genuine partnerships—without buying links or mass outreach.

### Days 61–90: refine instead of expanding blindly

**Outcome:** Choose the winning subtopic and decide whether a fourth content page is justified.

1. Compare homepage, template, and checklist query sets.
2. Resolve cannibalization by differentiating, merging, or strengthening internal-link signals.
3. Improve the page attracting the most relevant non-brand queries and meaningful actions.
4. Test one homepage message change centered on next-step ownership.
5. Review questions received from users, demos, or support and add only relevant FAQ material.
6. Create an ownership/handoff guide only if GSC or repeated first-party conversations show distinct intent.
7. Refresh the checklist or template instead of publishing generic weekly posts.
8. Document the next-quarter decision: expand, narrow, consolidate, or pause.
