**Composite: 2.83/5.00.**

Scheduling is a proven software category, but the broad product idea has no demonstrated reason for users to leave established products or free calendar features. A solo developer could build a basic scheduler, yet integrations, reliability, time-zone behavior, support, and distribution make a competitive horizontal product substantially harder than the UI suggests. Do not build a general Calendly alternative. Spend one to two weeks testing a narrow workflow-specific wedge.

## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem in user language:** “Arranging meetings takes too many messages, and existing schedulers do not quite match my workflow.”
- **Proposed solution:** A new scheduling tool that exposes availability and lets another person book a time.
- **Narrow ICP for validation:** Independent tutors and coaches who sell recurring, prepaid sessions across time zones.
- **Non-ICP:** General consumers, internal enterprise scheduling, medical practices, salons, and “anyone who attends meetings.”
- **Success signals:** At least five prospects independently describe the same costly scheduling problem; three currently pay for a workaround; two pay or provide a deposit for a concierge prototype.
- **Kill signals:** Prospects are satisfied with existing tools, only request cosmetic improvements, will not switch calendars, or expect the complete product for free.

## 6. Commercial value

**Mixed.** Scheduling can affect administrative time, attendance, and cash collection, but this report found no evidence that a defined buyer has a sufficiently painful, poorly served problem. The strongest possible commercial case is not “easier booking”; it is an outcome such as fewer unpaid sessions, faster client qualification, fewer time-zone errors, or less revenue lost to late cancellations. Which outcome attracts budget remains unproven.

## 7. Independent-developer fit

**Conditional.**

A concierge prototype or narrow booking layer is feasible for one developer. A dependable horizontal scheduling platform is less attractive because it can require multiple calendar providers, OAuth maintenance, time-zone and daylight-saving correctness, webhook reliability, notification delivery, payment handling, privacy work, and rapid support when bookings fail.

Distribution is the larger problem. Search and word-of-mouth are likely difficult for a generic scheduler. A viable solo path needs a concentrated vertical community and a workflow that incumbents handle poorly. Founder skills and existing audience are unknown.

## 8. Short-term return: 30–90 days

**Odds: Low.**

Define initial return as **two paying pilot customers or $100 in collected revenue**, not meaningful recurring revenue.

- **Best plausible path:** Sell a manually supported scheduling-and-payment workflow to one narrow professional niche before building calendar infrastructure.
- **Why odds are low:** No audience, validated pain, switching trigger, or acquisition channel was supplied or discovered.
- **What would falsify this judgment:** Ten interviews reveal repeated monetary loss from the same workflow, at least three prospects currently pay for fragmented workarounds, and two purchase a pilot.

## 9. Entry directions

### Primary wedge: recurring-session operations for independent tutors and coaches

- **Who:** Tutors and coaches serving clients across time zones and selling session packages.
- **Job to be done:** Sell a package, collect intake information, schedule recurring sessions, track remaining credits, and enforce rescheduling rules.
- **MVP shape:** A manually operated booking page plus package balance, payment link, confirmation, and rescheduling policy. Use existing calendar infrastructure initially.
- **Where to find the first ten users:** Two buyer communities or professional groups identified during the manual scan; direct outreach only where community rules permit it.
- **Fourteen-day metric:** Ten interviews, five repeated examples of the same workflow failure, three pilot commitments, and two paid deposits.

### Backup wedges

1. Interview scheduling and candidate-data collection for boutique recruiting agencies.
2. Qualification-first scheduling for independent consultants who want to screen leads before exposing availability.

### Explicitly do not start with

- A general-purpose Calendly clone.
- Enterprise team scheduling.
- Medical or other regulated appointment workflows.
- An all-in-one calendar client.
- AI scheduling until a non-AI workflow problem and payment intent are demonstrated.

## 10. Risks and open questions

- Is the problem painful enough to cause switching?
- Which outcome has monetary value: reduced administration, fewer no-shows, or faster payment?
- Do tutors and coaches already have adequate vertical products?
- Will buyers grant calendar permissions to an unknown vendor?
- Are recurring bookings and session credits common enough to define a market?
- Can the product avoid becoming a support-heavy integration business?
- Does the founder have access to a relevant buyer community?
- Can the wedge remain focused, or will every customer require different rules?

## 11. Next 48 hours

1. Run a 30–60 minute manual scan across Reddit buyer communities, X search from the last 90 days, HN Algolia, Product Hunt, and one review site. Capture ten linked statements, including at least three complaints and three “already solved” objections.
2. Interview five tutors or coaches using past-behavior questions: how they scheduled their last ten sessions, what failed, what they currently pay for, and when a cancellation last cost them money.
3. Offer a manually fulfilled package-booking pilot for a real price or refundable deposit. Continue only if at least two prospects pay or deposit; reject compliments and waitlist signups as payment evidence.
