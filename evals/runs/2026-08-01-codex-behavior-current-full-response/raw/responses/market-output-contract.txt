## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem in user language:** Support agents repeatedly reconstruct the set of tabs needed to handle a particular client, losing time and context between clients or shifts.
- **Proposed solution:** A Chrome extension that restores a named, client-specific tab workspace.
- **Narrow ICP:** Small B2B support teams whose agents switch among several client-specific browser workflows every day and whose administrators permit Chrome extensions.
- **Economic buyer:** Provisionally the support lead; this is unverified.
- **Non-ICP:** Individual users satisfied with bookmarks or Chrome’s native features; single-client agents; enterprises that prohibit extensions; workflows requiring restoration of authenticated application state.
- **Initial success condition:** A team uses restored client workspaces repeatedly and agrees to pay—not merely says the idea sounds useful.
- **Kill signals:** Users do not repeatedly invoke the workspace; Chrome-native features remain good enough; administrators reject installation; or no lead accepts a paid pilot.

## 6. Commercial value

**Verdict: Mixed.** The potential value is recovering repeated shift time and reducing omitted client context. At the upper reported loss, an agent could lose roughly 100 minutes across five shifts, but that extrapolation is illustrative—not observed evidence. The likely buyer is a support lead paying for consistency or saved labor, yet buyer authority and budget remain unverified.

The product will struggle if positioned as “better saved tabs.” Its commercial case must be “open the correct client operating context reliably, including during handoffs,” with demonstrable repeated time savings. It must also avoid claiming restoration of login state or customer data unless those capabilities are actually safe and feasible.

## 7. Independent-developer fit

**Verdict: Conditional.**

- **Build scope:** A local extension supporting named workspaces, one-click open/close, ordering, and basic import/export appears compatible with four weeks. This is a feasibility inference, not a tested estimate.
- **Scope hazard:** Shared workspaces introduce identity, synchronization, permissions, conflict handling, onboarding, and support. They should not be in the first validation build.
- **Support burden:** Browser updates, inaccessible URLs, authentication expectations, duplicate tabs, and extension permissions may generate disproportionate support.
- **Distribution:** No verified channel exists. The current community participants are the only supplied starting pool.
- **Policy risk:** Administrator approval is a gating dependency, not a later optimization.
- **Capital:** The $500 budget is adequate for a narrow local prototype and manual pilot, but poorly suited to building team infrastructure before demand is established.
- **Skills match:** Unknown.

## 8. Short-term return: 30–90 days

**Verdict: Low odds of reaching $500 MRR; plausible odds of learning whether a paid niche exists.**

- **Success metric:** The stated outcome is $500 MRR within 90 days. An earlier proof point should be one paid team pilot with repeated agent use and administrator approval.
- **Best plausible path:** Sell a manually onboarded pilot to support leads from the supplied community, using a local-first extension and charging within the unverified adjacent range or a clearly labeled team-minimum experiment.
- **Why the odds are low:** Payment is unproven, free substitutes are credible, extension approval may block adoption, and 42–72 seats are needed at the only supplied price range.
- **What falsifies the path:** No paid pilot after direct offers to qualified leads; fewer than half of testers use the restoration repeatedly during real shifts; measured savings are trivial; or administrators reject the required permissions.

A failed spike should stop or reshape the idea. It should not trigger a broader team-workspace build.

## 9. Entry direction

### Primary wedge: local-first client runbooks for small B2B support teams

- **Who:** Agents in small, extension-permitted B2B support teams who switch among multiple client-specific browser contexts daily.
- **Job to be done:** “When I switch to a client or receive a handoff, open the correct working set immediately without reconstructing it from memory.”
- **MVP:** Named client workspaces; ordered URL sets; one-click restore; duplicate-tab handling; local storage; simple export/import for manually sharing a template.
- **Deliberate boundary:** No accounts, cloud synchronization, analytics, customer-data capture, or automatic sharing in the spike.
- **Buyer hypothesis:** Support lead responsible for agent productivity.
- **Where to find the first users:** Only the supplied community participants and three reported support leads are currently supported by evidence.
- **Fourteen-day metric:** At least five qualified agents test real client workflows; at least three use the extension on three separate shifts; at least one lead accepts a paid pilot subject to administrator approval.

### Backup wedges

- **Individual power-user version:** Private client workspace restoration for consultants or support agents who control their own browsers.
- **Concierge team setup:** Manually configured, exportable workspace templates without cloud collaboration.

### Explicitly do not start with

- A general-purpose tab manager.
- Real-time shared team workspaces.
- Enterprise administration or integrations.
- Restoration of credentials, form state, or sensitive customer content.
- A four-week full build based only on feature requests.
