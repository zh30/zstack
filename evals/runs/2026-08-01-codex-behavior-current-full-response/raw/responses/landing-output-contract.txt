# 10-Day Smoke-Test Package

Working product name: **ClientTab**. Treat it as provisional until a trademark/domain check is completed.

Evidence limitation: web and browser access were unavailable for this run. The package therefore contains no externally verified market, competitor, keyword, ad-inventory, or current Buttondown/Plausible API findings. Confirm current provider capabilities and community rules before launch.

## 1. Smoke protocol

### Offer

A planned Chrome extension for hands-on support agents at small US SaaS companies. It saves a set of client-specific tabs as a workspace and restores that context in one click.

- Primary promise: **Restore each client’s support tabs in one click**
- Single CTA: **Join the early-access waitlist**
- Truthful price signal: **Planned pricing from $9/month**
- Stage: Unbuilt; demand is being tested before development
- Geography: United States
- Test window: **August 3–12, 2026**
- Paid-media cap: **$150 total**

### Hypothesis

If hands-on support agents at small US SaaS teams see a focused offer to restore each client’s working context in one click, qualified cold visitors will join an early-access waitlist at source-appropriate rates, and some subscribers will describe a recurring client-tab problem or credible willingness to pay from $9/month.

### Primary metric

For each traffic source separately:

```text
Confirmed waitlist conversion rate
= Buttondown double-opt-in-confirmed subscribers attributed to that source
  ÷ qualified unique landing-page visitors from that source
```

A qualified visitor is a US visitor arriving through a test UTM whose role or behavior plausibly matches customer support, customer success, implementation, or technical support at a small SaaS team. Bot traffic, internal testing, accidental clicks, and obvious job-seeker/vendor traffic are excluded and logged.

Never combine warm outreach with cold traffic. Report these as separate cohorts:

1. Warm personal outreach
2. Cold community traffic
3. Cold social traffic
4. Cold paid traffic

### Secondary metrics

- Landing view → CTA click rate
- Valid form attempts
- Buttondown-accepted subscriptions
- Double-opt-in confirmations
- Welcome-email opens, used only as a deliverability diagnostic
- Welcome-email replies
- Replies describing a recurring client-specific tab workflow
- Credible price intent: “I would try/pay at $9” or a request for paid access
- Cost per confirmed subscriber for paid traffic
- Unsubscribes and spam complaints

Do not treat clicks, attempted submissions, opens, or unconfirmed subscriptions as waitlist conversions.

### Sample plan

Ten days may not produce decision-grade samples. The test should aim for:

| Source | Visitor goal | Minimum before a source verdict | Conversion-count guardrail |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Warm personal outreach | 20 | 15 | At least 3 confirmations |
| Cold niche communities | 80 | 60 | At least 3 confirmations |
| Cold organic social | 60 | 50 | At least 2 confirmations |
| Cold paid traffic | 100 | 75 or $120 spent | At least 3 confirmations |

If a source misses its minimum, label its result **inconclusive**, not failed. Continue only the test—not product development—until the minimum is reached or distribution is shown to be impractical.

### Pre-registered source thresholds

| Source | Continue signal | Narrow/retest signal | Stop this acquisition path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm outreach | ≥20% confirmed CVR, ≥3 confirmations, and ≥2 substantive replies | 10–19.9% or confirmations without problem detail | <10% after 20 qualified visits |
| Cold niche communities | ≥8% confirmed CVR and ≥5 confirmations | 4–7.9%, or good clicks with weak confirmation | <4% after 60 qualified visits and no substantive replies |
| Cold organic social | ≥6% confirmed CVR and ≥3 confirmations | 3–5.9% | <3% after 50 qualified visits and no substantive replies |
| Cold paid | ≥4% confirmed CVR, ≥4 confirmations, and cost per confirmation ≤$37.50 | 2–3.9% or cost per confirmation >$37.50 | <2% after 75 qualified visits, or $120 spent with ≤1 confirmation |

These are directional pre-commitments, not universal benchmarks.

### Demand-quality threshold

A positive landing-page rate alone is insufficient. Before beginning an MVP, require:

- A cold-source threshold to pass; and
- At least **five substantive replies** across confirmed subscribers; and
- At least **three people** describing a recurring client-context workflow; and
- At least **two cold subscribers** expressing credible willingness to try the planned $9/month offer.

Warm responses cannot satisfy the cold requirement.

# 4. Implementation package

## Architecture

```text
Cloudflare Pages
  ├── Static single-page landing page
  ├── /api/waitlist Pages Function
  ├── Buttondown API
  └── Plausible analytics
```

Environment placeholders:

```text
BUTTONDOWN_API_KEY
BUTTONDOWN_LIST_OR_TAG_ID
PLAUSIBLE_DOMAIN
PUBLIC_SITE_URL
```

Store the Buttondown API key only as a Cloudflare secret. Never expose it in client JavaScript.

Because current documentation could not be checked, confirm Buttondown’s current API endpoint, double-opt-in behavior, metadata/tag support, duplicate response, and webhook or redirect options before implementation.

## Form capture

Fields sent to the Pages Function:

```json
{
  "email": "required",
  "workflow_note": "optional",
  "utm_source": "hidden",
  "utm_medium": "hidden",
  "utm_campaign": "hidden",
  "utm_content": "hidden",
  "utm_term": "hidden",
  "landing_variant": "A",
  "traffic_class": "derived server-side",
  "page_path": "/",
  "consent_version": "2026-08-03"
}
```

Implementation requirements:

- Validate email on client and server.
- Add a hidden honeypot and basic rate limiting.
- Never send `email` or `workflow_note` to Plausible.
- Derive `traffic_class` from an allowlist; do not trust arbitrary client input.
- Store UTMs in first-party session storage and copy them into the form.
- Use Buttondown double opt-in for list quality and defensible confirmations.
- Preserve source attribution in Buttondown metadata or tags.
- If arbitrary metadata is unsupported, use separate provider-native tags/forms for each cohort.
- Test one valid, one invalid, one duplicate, and one bot-like submission.
- Confirm that a real subscriber record retains the expected source before buying traffic.

### Traffic-class allowlist

```text
warm_outreach
cold_community
cold_social
cold_paid
direct_unknown
```

`direct_unknown` is reported separately and cannot be used to pass a source threshold.

## Response and success integrity

The Pages Function should:

1. Validate the request.
2. Send the subscriber to Buttondown.
3. Wait for Buttondown’s response.
4. Return `accepted_pending_confirmation` only when Buttondown accepts it.
5. Return a non-success state for invalid or rejected requests.

Do not show “You’re on the list” from a submit handler alone.

The primary conversion is the later double-opt-in confirmation. Preferred implementation:

- Buttondown confirmation redirect goes to `/confirmed?source_token=...`; or
- A provider webhook records the confirmation and sends a server-side Plausible event.

If neither is supported, Buttondown’s confirmed-subscriber export is the conversion source of truth. Reconcile it daily by source; Plausible will then show funnel diagnostics, not final conversion totals.

## Plausible events

Use these event names and properties, subject to the capabilities of the current Plausible plan:

| Event | Fires when | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| `Waitlist CTA Click` | Any primary CTA scrolls/focuses the form | `source`, `medium`, `campaign`, `content`, `variant`, `traffic_class`, `placement` |
| `Waitlist Attempt` | A locally valid request is sent | Same, excluding all personal/form text |
| `Waitlist Accepted` | Pages Function confirms provider acceptance | Same |
| `Waitlist Confirmed` | Provider-verifiable double-opt-in confirmation occurs | Same; preferred server-side or confirmation-page event |

Do not report `Waitlist Attempt` or `Waitlist Accepted` as confirmed waitlist conversion.

# 8. Operating and decision rules

## Daily checks

Before interpreting demand:

1. Are visitors geographically and professionally plausible?
2. Are UTMs retained in confirmed Buttondown records?
3. Are confirmation emails being delivered?
4. Are mobile CTAs and form responses functioning?
5. Are bots, internal tests, duplicates, and invalid addresses excluded?
6. Are warm and cold cohorts still separate?

## Day-10 decisions

### Proceed to a thin MVP

Proceed only if:

- At least one cold source passes its continue threshold;
- The demand-quality threshold is satisfied;
- No operational defect explains the result;
- The primary use case remains specifically client-context restoration.

The thin MVP should test only:

```text
Save named tab workspace → list workspaces → restore workspace in one click
```

### Extend the smoke test

Extend traffic—not development—when:

- A source looks promising but has not reached its minimum;
- Confirmed CVR passes but fewer than five substantive replies exist;
- Paid targeting is qualified but the sample is too small;
- Attribution or email confirmation failed for part of the window.

Freeze the original ten-day cohort and label the extension separately.

### Rewrite and retest

Rewrite the hero once when:

- CTA clicks are low but visitors appear qualified;
- Replies reveal a sharper phrase, such as “resume an escalation” rather than “switch clients”;
- One or more cold sources land in their narrow/retest band.

Change only the hero and subheadline. Preserve audience, CTA, price, and attribution so the comparison remains interpretable.

### Interview before building

Do not build yet when conversion passes but replies are silent or vague. Conduct five short interviews with cold-source subscribers about:

- The last time they reconstructed client context
- Tabs and systems involved
- Frequency
- Existing workaround
- Consequences of getting it wrong
- Whether they currently pay for adjacent tools

### Stop or reshape

Park the concept when:

- Two cold sources reach their minimum and both fall below their stop thresholds;
- There are no substantive cold replies;
- The offer has received one controlled copy retest;
- Tracking and delivery are functioning.

This disproves the tested offer/audience/channel combination—not every conceivable tab-management product.

### Strong price signal

A credible cold reply asking to pay, receive an invoice, or obtain paid access is a high-value lead. Follow up individually to understand the job and constraints. One such message is not proof of scalable demand.
