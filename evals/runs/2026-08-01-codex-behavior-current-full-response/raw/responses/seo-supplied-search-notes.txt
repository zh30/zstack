# 90-day SEO plan: ownership-aware client onboarding

## Executive summary

- **Market:** English, United States
- **Primary cluster:** `agency client onboarding workflow`
- **Primary page:** Homepage `/`
- **Audience:** Owners and operations leads at small agencies
- **Core positioning:** A client onboarding workflow that makes the owner and next action visible—not another passive checklist.
- **Primary conversion:** Use the product’s current highest-intent action—trial, demo, or waitlist—and track it consistently.
- **Capacity:** Six founder hours per week, approximately 72 hours across three 30-day phases.
- **Strategy:** Concentrate the site around one commercially relevant, agency-specific workflow cluster. Rewrite the homepage as its conversion page, strengthen the existing checklist article, and add only two supporting resources: a usable workflow template and an ownership/next-step guide.

## Product and search context

### Searcher and job-to-be-done

The likely searcher is a small-agency owner or operations lead whose onboarding work spans sales, account management, delivery, and the client. Their immediate jobs are:

- Define a repeatable onboarding process.
- Know who owns every task and handoff.
- See what the next step is and why it is blocked.
- Reduce chasing, missed inputs, and dormant checklists.
- Evaluate whether a template is enough or workflow software is needed.

The supplied community phrasing—“who owns the next step?” and “why don’t checklists get done?”—provides the strongest differentiation. It should shape copy and content, but it does not yet prove search demand or willingness to buy.

### Cannibalization rules

- The homepage owns **workflow, process, product, and automation** intent.
- The checklist article owns the **task-list/checklist** intent.
- The template page owns **template, example, and copyable workflow** intent.
- The ownership guide owns **owners, accountability, handoffs, blockers, and unfinished checklists**.
- Do not publish separate pages for “agency onboarding process,” “client onboarding workflow software,” or “client onboarding automation” during the first 90 days.
- If two pages begin receiving impressions for the same query family, choose one owner and redirect, merge, or reposition the other.

## Homepage brief

### Search and conversion

- **Primary keyword:** `agency client onboarding workflow`
- **Intent:** Commercial/mixed
- **Title:** `Client Onboarding Workflow for Small Agencies | [Brand]`
- **H1:** `Know Who Owns Every Client Onboarding Step`
- **Suggested meta description:** `Run agency client onboarding with a visible owner, next step, and status for every handoff. See how [Brand] turns checklists into an accountable workflow.`
- **Canonical URL:** `/`
- **Primary CTA:** Use the existing trial, demo, or waitlist action.
- **Secondary CTA:** `See the onboarding workflow` or `Use the template`.

### Recommended content order

1. **Hero**
   - State the audience and outcome immediately.
   - Show owner, next step, and status in the product visual.
   - Avoid generic claims such as “streamline your business.”

2. **Problem**
   - Checklists describe work but do not ensure ownership.
   - Agency onboarding crosses internal roles and client responsibilities.
   - A stalled handoff needs a visible owner and next action.

3. **How the workflow works**
   - Start from a reusable process.
   - Assign an owner to every step.
   - Route internal and client handoffs.
   - Surface blocked or overdue work.
   - Confirm completion and begin delivery.

4. **Ownership model**
   - Explain the difference between assignee, responsible owner, client dependency, and observer—only where these concepts match the product.
   - Show what happens when the next step changes hands.

5. **Agency-specific example**
   - Contract signed.
   - Deposit or payment confirmed.
   - Intake information requested.
   - Access and assets collected.
   - Kickoff scheduled.
   - Delivery team briefed.

6. **Checklist versus workflow**
   - Checklist: what must happen.
   - Workflow: what happens next, who owns it, and what is blocked.
   - Link to the rewritten checklist and ownership guide.

7. **Automation**
   - Describe only capabilities the product actually has.
   - Cover reminders, task creation, status changes, and handoffs where truthful.
   - Do not imply integrations or AI features that do not exist.

8. **Template preview**
   - Show part of the workflow before asking for signup.
   - Link to the full template page.

9. **Trust and proof**
   - Founder identity, product screenshots, company contact information, and privacy/terms.
   - Add testimonials, agency examples, or outcome data only when genuine.

10. **FAQ**
    - What is an agency client onboarding workflow?
    - Who should own client onboarding?
    - How is a workflow different from a checklist?
    - Can onboarding handoffs be automated?
    - What should a small-agency onboarding process include?

Use FAQ structured data only when these questions and answers are visibly present and eligible under current search-engine guidance.

## Measurement plan

### Setup in the first week

- Verify the canonical domain in Google Search Console.
- Submit the XML sitemap.
- Record the date each page is published or materially changed.
- Configure analytics events for:
  - Homepage primary CTA
  - Template use/download
  - Signup, demo request, or waitlist completion
- Store landing page and initial referrer with the conversion where technically and legally appropriate.
- Create one weekly worksheet containing page, query, country, device, impressions, clicks, and conversions.

### Weekly review

Use an English-US GSC filter and separate branded from non-branded queries. Review:

- Whether Google has discovered and indexed each target page.
- Relevant non-brand impressions by cluster and landing page.
- Which queries Google associates with each page.
- Whether two pages compete for the same query family.
- Organic CTA actions and completed conversions.
- Irrelevant impressions that indicate positioning drift.

Do not treat average position alone as success; it is unstable and context-dependent.

### Decision rules

- **Indexing failure:** Fix technical/indexability issues before producing more content.
- **Relevant impressions but weak clicks:** Rework title and description to better match observed intent.
- **Clicks but no CTA activity:** Improve message match, product explanation, proof, and CTA.
- **CTA activity but no completed conversion:** Inspect the signup/demo flow rather than publishing more articles.
- **Query overlap:** Consolidate or differentiate pages immediately.
- **No relevant impressions by day 60:** Manually validate US SERPs and reconsider the phrase/page match before expanding.
- **Clear relevant query growth or organic conversions:** Expand only the page and subtopic producing that evidence.

No traffic, ranking, or conversion forecasts should be set before a baseline exists.

## Explicit avoid list

- `client onboarding software` and similarly broad category head terms: supplied notes suggest vendor-dominated SERPs and weak early-site winnability.
- `CRM for agencies`, `client portal software`, and `project management software`: broader product categories with potentially different intent.
- Separate pages for workflow, process, automation, system, and software synonyms: high cannibalization risk.
- City/state pages: no local-service intent has been established.
- Industry variants such as marketing, design, web, SEO, and creative agency pages before GSC proves distinct demand.
- Competitor “alternative” and “versus” pages without genuine product knowledge and comparison value.
- Dozens of checklist permutations: strengthen the existing article instead.
- Programmatic template pages with near-duplicate content.
- Generic high-volume business content disconnected from onboarding ownership.
- Unsupported “best,” “#1,” time-saving, ROI, customer-count, integration, or automation claims.
- AI-generated content at a cadence the founder cannot review and maintain.
- Purchased backlinks, link exchanges at scale, private blog networks, doorway pages, hidden text, or keyword stuffing.
- Premature redesign or speculative Core Web Vitals work without evidence of a real problem.
