# 90-day SEO plan: ownership-aware client onboarding for small agencies

## Executive summary

- **Primary cluster:** `agency client onboarding workflow`
- **Primary page:** Homepage `/`
- **Primary intent:** Mixed commercial and informational
- **Positioning:** A client onboarding workflow for small agencies that makes the next owner, handoff, and due date explicit.
- **Confidence:** Low–medium. Product relevance is strong, but demand, current SERPs, and difficulty were not independently verified.
- **Strategy:** Build one narrow cluster around agency onboarding workflows. Use the homepage for category and product intent, then support it with a practical template, an upgraded checklist, and one ownership-focused guide.
- **Primary conversion:** Start a trial, join a waitlist, or request access—use whichever is currently real.
- **Capacity:** Six founder hours per week; approximately 72 hours over 90 days.

### Evidence limit

Browsing, SERP inspection, GSC, backlink data, and paid keyword tools were unavailable. The supplied autocomplete-style phrases, community wording, and SERP descriptions are unverified inputs—not observed results. Therefore, no search volumes, traffic forecasts, or ranking claims are included.

## 1. Product and search context

### Product one-liner

An ownership-aware client onboarding workflow that helps small agencies see who owns the next step, what is due, and where a client handoff is blocked.

### Searcher

Small-agency founders, operations leads, account managers, and project managers responsible for turning a signed client into an active engagement.

### Jobs to be done

- Standardize client onboarding without adopting a full CRM or portal.
- Assign every onboarding action to an internal owner or client.
- Prevent stalled handoffs and uncompleted checklists.
- Reuse a workflow across clients.
- Automate reminders or transitions where appropriate.

## 4. Homepage brief

### Search and conversion elements

- **Suggested title:** `Client Onboarding Workflow for Small Agencies | [Brand]`
- **H1:** `Keep every client onboarding step owned and moving`
- **Primary CTA:** `Build your onboarding workflow` or the closest truthful product action
- **Secondary CTA:** `Get the free workflow template`
- **Meta description:** `Run a repeatable client onboarding workflow with clear owners, due dates, and handoffs. Help your small agency see who needs to act next.`

### Recommended page structure

1. **Hero**
   - State the audience and outcome immediately.
   - Show the ownership-aware workflow in a real product screenshot.
   - Explain “ownership-aware” in plain language rather than treating it as a known category.

2. **Problem**
   - A checklist records tasks but does not ensure action.
   - Handoffs stall when nobody knows whether the agency or client owns the next step.
   - Avoid unsupported claims such as percentages of projects delayed.

3. **How the workflow works**
   - Start from a reusable process.
   - Assign each step to an agency teammate or client.
   - Add due dates and prerequisites.
   - Surface the current owner and blocked step.
   - Reuse the workflow for the next client.

4. **Ownership and handoffs**
   - Answer: “Who owns the next step?”
   - Cover reassignment, client dependencies, notifications, and overdue actions—only where supported by the product.

5. **Agency onboarding process example**
   - Contract signed.
   - Intake form and access collection.
   - Internal kickoff.
   - Client kickoff.
   - First deliverable or activation.
   - Give every example step an owner and completion condition.

6. **Template preview**
   - Show several real steps.
   - Link to the standalone template page.

7. **Automation**
   - Describe actual triggers, reminders, or integrations.
   - If the product lacks automation, frame this as visibility and repeatability—not “automated onboarding.”

8. **Fit boundaries**
   - Best for small agencies that need structured handoffs.
   - Explain whether this replaces or complements a CRM, project manager, or client portal.

9. **FAQ**
   - What is an agency client onboarding workflow?
   - How is a workflow different from a checklist?
   - Who should own client onboarding?
   - Can client handoffs be automated?
   - What should be included in an onboarding template?

10. **Final CTA**
    - Repeat one primary product action.
    - Offer the template only as the lower-commitment alternative.

### Internal linking

- Homepage → template, checklist, ownership guide, pricing.
- Template → homepage product section and checklist.
- Checklist → template and ownership guide.
- Ownership guide → homepage and template.
- Pricing → homepage feature explanation.
- Every supporting page should have a contextual product CTA, not merely a footer link.

## 5. Supporting-page briefs

### Workflow template

- **URL:** `/client-onboarding-workflow-template/`
- **Title:** `Client Onboarding Workflow Template for Small Agencies`
- **H1:** `A client onboarding workflow template with clear owners`
- **Format:** Useful HTML template first; optional downloadable or copyable version second.
- **Must cover:** Workflow stages, task owner, agency/client responsibility, due date, prerequisite, status, and definition of done.
- **CTA:** Copy the template into the product.
- **Boundary:** Do not make it another general “what is client onboarding?” article.

### Existing checklist article

- **Primary target:** `client onboarding checklist for agencies`
- **Suggested title:** `Client Onboarding Checklist for Agencies: Owners and Due Dates`
- **H1:** `A client onboarding checklist your agency can actually complete`
- **Must cover:** Checklist steps, owner column, due dates, blocked states, client-owned items, and why static checklists stall.
- **CTA:** Turn the checklist into an active workflow.
- **Boundary:** The article owns checklist intent; the template page owns workflow-template intent.

### Ownership guide

- **URL:** `/guides/client-onboarding-ownership/`
- **Title:** `Who Owns the Next Step in Client Onboarding?`
- **H1:** `Make ownership explicit throughout client onboarding`
- **Must cover:** Accountable owner versus contributor, agency versus client tasks, handoff rules, escalation, reassignment, and examples.
- **CTA:** See the ownership-aware workflow.
- **Boundary:** Focus on responsibility and handoffs, not a second full checklist.

## 6. Technical baseline

Because the site and code were not inspectable, treat each item as a verification task rather than a diagnosed defect.

| Area | Required baseline |
|---|---|
| Indexability | Key marketing pages return `200`, contain crawlable HTML, and have no accidental `noindex` or authentication requirement |
| Canonicals | Each page has one self-referencing canonical using the preferred HTTPS hostname |
| Robots | Allow public marketing routes; reference the sitemap; do not block required rendering assets |
| Sitemap | Include canonical public pages only; update automatically when pages ship |
| Rendering | Titles, H1s, body copy, and links must exist in rendered HTML without requiring user interaction |
| Metadata | Give every mapped page a unique title and meta description |
| Status handling | Use real `404` responses for missing URLs; redirect retired duplicates to the surviving relevant page |
| Mobile | Verify navigation, screenshots, tables, forms, and CTAs on small screens |
| Performance | Record a baseline before optimization; compress product images and avoid unnecessary third-party scripts |
| Language | Set the document language to English; add `hreflang` only if localized equivalents exist |
| Structured data | Use `Organization`, `WebSite`, `BreadcrumbList`, and `Article` only where accurate; use FAQ markup only for visible FAQ content and current eligibility |
| Analytics | Install privacy-appropriate analytics and record CTA/template events |
| Search Console | Verify the domain, submit the sitemap, and monitor indexing and query data |
| Security | Use HTTPS consistently and prevent mixed-content resources |

### Trust baseline

- Publish an About page identifying the founder or company.
- Add accessible contact information.
- Link privacy policy and terms from signup, template capture, and site footer.
- Use real product screenshots and examples.
- Explain whether client data is stored and how it is protected.
- Do not invent customers, testimonials, integrations, certifications, or productivity results.

## 7. Measurement plan

### Setup

Create a simple weekly sheet with:

- Page URL
- Target query and intent
- Indexing status
- Non-brand GSC impressions
- Non-brand clicks
- Query-to-page match
- Organic CTA actions
- Template copies or downloads
- Organic signups or waitlist submissions
- Notes on irrelevant queries or cannibalization

Use UTM parameters only for off-site promotion. Preserve the organic source in analytics rather than overwriting it.

### Decision signals

| Time | Signal | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| First 30 days | Pages indexed and GSC beginning to expose real query wording | Fix indexing first; revise headings only when queries reveal a clearer match |
| By day 60 | Relevant non-brand impressions on one or more cluster pages | Expand sections and links around the phrases already receiving impressions |
| By day 60 | Two pages appear for the same intent | Consolidate, differentiate, or redirect; do not let both drift |
| By day 90 | Relevant clicks or organic conversions appear | Prioritize the winning page and its adjacent long tails |
| By day 90 | Indexed page has no relevant visibility | Check intent and SERPs before rewriting; consolidate or pause if the opportunity is unsupported |
| Any time | Traffic arrives for broad CRM/portal intent but does not engage | Tighten agency and ownership language rather than broadening the page |

Do not set traffic or conversion forecasts before there is a baseline. SEO evidence can validate acquisition language; it cannot establish product-market fit, willingness to pay, or retention.

## 8. Founder operating model

Keep the weekly six-hour allocation deliberately small:

- **Three hours:** Create or improve one priority page section.
- **One hour:** Add product truth—screenshots, examples, template steps, or FAQ answers.
- **One hour:** Technical checks and internal links.
- **One hour:** Review GSC and analytics, record decisions, and perform a limited manual US SERP check once browsing becomes available.

Do not attempt weekly generic blog publishing.

## 9. 30/60/90-day backlog

### Days 1–30: establish the cluster

**Outcome:** A technically measurable homepage and two useful cluster assets.

1. Verify indexability, canonicals, robots, sitemap, analytics, and GSC.
2. Rewrite the homepage around `agency client onboarding workflow`.
3. Preserve and expand the existing checklist article.
4. Publish the workflow-template page with a genuinely usable template.
5. Add contextual internal links across homepage, pricing, checklist, and template.
6. Add About, privacy, and terms pages if absent.
7. Record a no-metrics baseline: indexed URLs, page targets, CTA events, and current query availability.
8. Manually verify the primary and P0 SERPs when research access becomes available before changing the architecture.

### Days 31–60: deepen the ownership wedge

**Outcome:** The cluster explains why ownership matters and begins receiving observable query data.

1. Publish the ownership guide.
2. Add a clear owners-and-handoffs section to the homepage.
3. Add real screenshots or a short product walkthrough.
4. Review GSC weekly for:
   - Workflow versus process wording.
   - Checklist and template modifiers.
   - Agency-specific versus generic traffic.
   - Queries about owners, responsibility, next steps, and handoffs.
5. Improve titles and introductions only where observed queries indicate mismatch.
6. Investigate any homepage/checklist/template cannibalization.
7. Conduct limited, non-spam outreach to relevant partners or agency operators who would genuinely use the template; seek feedback first, not links.

### Days 61–90: consolidate and expand from evidence

**Outcome:** More effort goes to pages demonstrating relevant visibility or conversion behavior.

1. Refresh the page with the strongest relevant impressions, clicks, or product actions.
2. Add one long-tail section or article only if GSC or verified SERPs support it.
3. Decide whether `client onboarding automation for agencies` deserves a separate page:
   - Create it only if query data shows distinct automation intent and the product has substantive automation.
   - Otherwise retain it as a homepage section.
4. Consolidate or redirect overlapping content.
5. Improve template-to-product and article-to-product conversion paths.
6. Compare organic visitors’ template use, CTA actions, and signups with other acquisition sources.
7. Produce the next 90-day backlog from observed query-to-page performance, not from a larger speculative keyword list.
