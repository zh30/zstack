## Cross-interview patterns

### G1 — Pain

- Two strong recurring-pain observations: repeated 45-minute manual copying and weekly bookkeeping failures.
- A third participant pays an assistant, suggesting operational burden, but the summary lacks a concrete incident or frequency.
- One participant has no recent pain.
- The marketer’s current automation shows a workflow exists, but not that it is failing.

The supported problem is narrower than generic onboarding: recurring cross-tool coordination that consumes labor or fails operationally.

### G2 — Existing cost

Three independent participants already absorb tangible cost:

- Zapier plus 45 minutes of manual work.
- Paid assistant labor.
- Paid VA labor.

This supports willingness to spend on the problem category. It does not yet establish a viable price for a new product or identify who controls the budget.

### G3 — Switching

Positive signals:

- The agency PM abandoned an attempted template and requested a pilot.
- The marketer has switched tools before.

Material barriers:

- Unclear workflow ownership.
- Migration effort.
- Sensitive-file handling.
- CSV portability.

These barriers are not peripheral. A product that merely automates steps without solving ownership, migration, and data boundaries may reproduce the reasons existing approaches fail.

## Decision

**Verdict: Advance — but only to a narrow, paid concierge pilot, not a generalized SaaS build.**

The agency PM is the best entry wedge because that interview contains the clearest combination of recurring pain, measurable time cost, current tool spend, failed prior solution, and a concrete next-step request.

Provisional hypothesis:

> Agency PMs running recurring client onboarding across multiple tools will adopt a workflow that reduces manual copying while making task ownership explicit.

Do not yet broaden this to consultants, bookkeepers, or marketers. Their migration, sensitive-file, and portability requirements should be treated as open research questions, not launch scope.

## Concrete downstream handoff

### Immediate pilot

Owner: founder.

Within seven days, return to the agency PM and convert the request into a real pilot commitment:

1. Schedule a workflow-mapping session using one actual upcoming onboarding.
2. Record the tools involved, current 45-minute baseline, handoffs, owner of each step, and information that must not be copied.
3. Run a concierge-assisted solution before building broad integrations.
4. Ask for a concrete commitment: access to a real workflow, a scheduled onboarding date, and a paid pilot or deposit.
5. Recruit two additional cold agency PMs with the same recurring, cross-tool trigger.

Pilot scope:

- One onboarding workflow.
- Explicit owner and status for every handoff.
- Minimal cross-tool data movement.
- Manual or CSV-assisted migration where necessary.
- No storage or transfer of sensitive files until security requirements are understood.
- CSV export as a portability safeguard, not as proof of the main value proposition.

Success threshold:

- Three qualified agency PMs complete workflow reviews.
- At least two run the solution on real onboardings.
- The original 45-minute copying workflow falls to 15 minutes or less without creating ownership errors.
- At least one participant pays or places a deposit, and at least one asks to use it for a second onboarding.

Reshape trigger:

- Pain is confirmed but agencies refuse workflow access because migration, ownership, or permissions dominate.
- In that case, reshape toward onboarding orchestration and ownership visibility rather than automatic data synchronization.

Park trigger:

- Fewer than two of three qualified agency PMs run a real onboarding, nobody makes a financial commitment, or the manual work is tolerated once implementation requirements become concrete.

### Landing-smoke input after the pilot is scheduled

Use one CTA: **Apply for a hands-on onboarding workflow pilot.**

The copy may safely claim that the pilot targets repeated cross-tool copying and unclear handoffs. It must not claim proven security, universal integrations, automatic migration, or time savings until those results are observed.

No “top user quotes” can be handed off because the inputs contain no verbatim quotes. Use clearly labeled paraphrases and replace them with transcript language in the next round.

### PRD boundary

Only write a thin pilot PRD after a participant commits a live workflow. Initial non-goals should include:

- Universal client-onboarding support across professions.
- Sensitive-file storage.
- Fully automatic migration.
- A large integration catalog.
- Bookkeeping-specific compliance.
- Treating CSV preference or pitch compliments as validated demand.
