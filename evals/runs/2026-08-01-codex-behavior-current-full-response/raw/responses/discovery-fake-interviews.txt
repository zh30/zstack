Screen candidates by asking:

### Interview guide: 25–40 minutes

Do not begin by describing the product.

1. “Tell me about the last time this happened.”
2. “What triggered it?”
3. “Walk me through exactly what you did.”
4. “Which tools, people, or workarounds were involved?”
5. “How often has this happened recently?”
6. “What did the last incident cost in time, money, delay, or risk?”
7. “What have you already tried to improve?”
8. “Did you pay for anything or request budget?”
9. “Have you changed solutions before? What caused the change?”
10. “What prevents you from changing the current process?”
11. “Who else participates in the decision?”
12. “What happens if nothing changes?”
13. “Who else has dealt with this recently?”

Only at the end, if useful, give a neutral 60-second product description. Treat praise and “I would use it” as weak opinions—not evidence.

### Debrief every interview

Record:

- Specific past incidents and approximate dates.
- Current workflow, tools, and alternatives.
- Existing time or money spent.
- Failed attempts and prior switches.
- Buyer, budget owner, and switching barriers.
- Verbatim quotations.
- Warm or cold recruitment source.
- Gate result: support, neutral, or contradict.
- Any leading questions, pitching, or future-tense answers.
- Pitch-leakage score from 0–5; lower is better.

Segment respondents into:

- **Bleeding and paying:** recurring pain plus demonstrated expenditure.
- **Bleeding, not paying:** real pain but no meaningful action or cost.
- **Polite only:** compliments, hypotheticals, or vague stories.

### Decision after ten real interviews

- **Advance to a smoke test or very thin MVP:** repeated recent pain, meaningful existing cost, and credible switching behavior appear across the narrow segment.
- **Reshape:** pain is real, but the customer segment, trigger, buyer, or proposed wedge is wrong.
- **Park:** after ten honest conversations, stories are infrequent, workarounds cost little, and nobody has tried to change.

Do not green-light a large build unless all three gates have primary evidence:

- **G1 Pain:** recent, repeated incidents with material consequences.
- **G2 Spend:** money, labor, time, risk, or operational effort already spent.
- **G3 Switch:** active search, prior switching, accessible budget owner, or concrete willingness to take a costly next step.
