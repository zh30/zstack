## Interview guide

Do not begin by explaining the product.

1. **Context**

   - What does a normal week involving `[workflow]` look like?
   - Who else is involved?
   - Which tools are systems of record?

2. **Last incident—G1**

   - Tell me about the last time `[trigger]` happened.
   - What led up to it?
   - What did you do next?
   - How long did it take?
   - What was the consequence?
   - When had it happened before?

3. **Current solution and cost—G2**

   - Walk me through how you handle it today.
   - Which tools, spreadsheets, contractors, or internal processes are involved?
   - What have you tried and abandoned?
   - What does the current approach cost in money or staff time?
   - Where does that budget come from?

4. **Switching evidence—G3**

   - When did you last replace a tool or process like this?
   - What triggered the change?
   - What made the switch difficult?
   - Who approved it?
   - What happens if the current problem remains unchanged for another year?

5. **Optional final 60-second idea mention**

   Only after collecting the behavioral stories, describe the idea in one plain sentence. Ask:

   - Does this correspond to anything you already do?
   - What would prevent this from fitting the current workflow?
   - Who else should I interview?

Do not ask “Would you use it?”, “Do you like it?”, or “How much would you pay?” Hypothetical enthusiasm is not buying evidence.

## What to capture after every call

Record:

- Recruitment source and warm/cold status
- A concrete recent incident
- Frequency and consequences
- Named tools, workarounds, and costs
- Abandoned alternatives
- Budget owner
- Previous switching behavior and barriers
- Short verbatim quotations
- G1/G2/G3: support, neutral, or contradict
- Whether you pitched or asked leading/future-tense questions
- Pitch-leakage score from 0–5, where lower is better

Classify each interviewee as:

- **Bleeding and paying:** recurring pain plus existing expenditure
- **Bleeding, not paying:** real pain but no meaningful action or cost
- **Polite only:** opinions, compliments, or hypothetical interest without behavior

## Decision rule after ten interviews

Consider a thin smoke test or narrowly scoped MVP only if:

- G1 receives repeated support from independent interviewees describing recent, costly incidents.
- G2 shows that a coherent segment already spends meaningful money, time, labor, or risk.
- G3 shows credible switching behavior, reachable budget owners, and manageable adoption barriers.
- Evidence comes from multiple cold prospects—not merely enthusiastic friends.

Then score each gate from 1–5:

- **4–5:** repeated behavioral support
- **3:** mixed evidence, but a plausible segment exists
- **1–2:** weak, isolated, or contradictory evidence

Decision:

- **Advance:** all three gates are strongly supported for one narrow segment.
- **Reshape:** pain is credible, but the ICP, trigger, solution wedge, or switching path is wrong.
- **Park:** honest interviews still show weak pain, no existing cost, or implausible switching.

Even an Advance verdict supports only a smoke test or thin MVP—not a large build.
