I can’t build a plan that uses doorway pages, hidden text, bought ranking links, or mass-produced thin city pages. Those tactics are deceptive, can create indexing or penalty risk, and rarely form a durable acquisition channel.

Below is the fastest defensible alternative. It assumes you provide a real, location-dependent service. Replace placeholders such as `{service}` and `{primary city}` with verified product language.

# SEO execution plan

## 1. Decision and evidence limits

- Primary conversion: qualified enquiry, booking, or signup.
- Site authority: unknown.
- Product, market, URL, code, competitors, and existing analytics: not supplied.
- Live SERP, keyword-volume, and competitor research: blocked because web access is unavailable.
- Demand and difficulty therefore remain `unknown`; no ranking or traffic forecast is supportable.
- If location does not materially affect availability, delivery, regulation, pricing, or customer proof, do not create city pages at all.

## 2. Replace the requested tactics

- **Doorway pages → useful service-area pages:** Every indexed location page must answer a genuinely location-specific need and work as a visitor’s final destination.
- **Purchased ranking links → legitimate promotion:** Use relevant directories, associations, partner resources, original research, local PR, and earned editorial coverage. Mark paid placements `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`.
- **Hidden text → visible, useful copy:** Important content must be readable by users. Accordions are acceptable for usability, but not for concealing keyword blocks.
- **Mass city pages → evidence-gated expansion:** Publish one excellent pilot page first. Expand only into places you actually serve and only when each page contains substantively distinct information.

## 3. Narrow keyword hypothesis

Primary cluster: local transactional demand for `{service}` in one real service area.

### Primary keyword

- **Keyword:** `{service} in {primary city}`
- **Intent:** Transactional/commercial
- **Target:** `/locations/{primary-city}/`
- **Demand:** Unknown
- **Difficulty:** Unknown
- **Reason to test:** Directly connects local availability with conversion intent, assuming the service is genuinely location-dependent.

### Secondary terms

Map these to the same page unless SERP evidence later demonstrates distinct intent:

- `{service} {primary city}`
- `best {service} in {primary city}`
- `{service} near me`

Do not create separate near-duplicate pages for these variants.

### Long-tail backlog

- **P0:** `how much does {service} cost in {primary city}`
- **P0:** `{service} pricing {primary city}`
- **P0:** `{service} for {audience} in {primary city}`
- **P1:** `same-day {service} {primary city}` — only if reliably offered
- **P1:** `licensed {service} {primary city}` — only if licensed and relevant
- **P2:** `{service} in {neighborhood}` — cover within the city page before considering a separate URL
- **P2:** `emergency {service} {primary city}` — only if genuine 24/7 emergency service exists

These are hypotheses derived from common search-language patterns, not observed demand.

## 4. Page ownership

- `/locations/{primary-city}/` owns the core city-service intent and “near me” variants.
- `/pricing/` owns general pricing intent; add a city-specific section only when prices genuinely differ.
- `/service-areas/` lists verified coverage and links to substantial location pages.
- `/customers/{city}-{outcome}/` can host authentic local case studies without competing for the primary query.
- The homepage owns the brand/category proposition, not every city keyword.

One primary intent should have one canonical URL. Merge or redirect pages that compete for the same query.

## 5. P0 location-page brief

**Title:** `{Service} in {Primary City} | {Specific differentiator} | {Brand}`  
**H1:** `{Service} in {Primary City}`  
**Slug:** `/locations/{primary-city}/`  
**Meta angle:** Availability, differentiated benefit, and an honest conversion proposition.

The page should visibly include:

- Exact services available in that city.
- Real coverage boundaries and response or delivery constraints.
- Location-specific pricing factors, regulations, or logistics.
- Named local staff, office, or operational details when real.
- Original photographs, completed work, testimonials, or case studies.
- City-specific FAQs drawn from actual enquiries.
- Clear contact information and booking eligibility.
- Links to pricing, relevant service detail, proof, policies, and adjacent legitimate service areas.
- A single CTA such as “Check availability in `{primary city}`.”

A changed city name, generic paragraph, stock photo, and identical FAQ are not sufficient differentiation.

## 6. Publication gate for additional cities

An indexed city page must satisfy all of these:

1. The business genuinely serves the location.
2. The location creates a distinct customer need or operational answer.
3. There is unique proof, coverage information, pricing context, regulation, logistics, or customer evidence.
4. The page offers a complete next step without redirecting users through an intermediate doorway.
5. Its copy has been manually reviewed for accuracy.
6. It does not compete with an existing page for the same intent.
7. It appears in the sitemap and internal navigation only after passing review.

Keep incomplete generated pages unpublished or `noindex`. If several cities cannot support distinct pages, consolidate them into one useful regional service-area page.

## 7. Authority plan without link manipulation

Prioritize:

- Accurate profiles on legitimate industry and local directories.
- Supplier, integration, chamber, and professional-association listings.
- Original data, calculators, templates, or local benchmark reports worth citing.
- Authentic customer case studies jointly promoted with permission.
- Expert commentary for relevant journalists and industry publications.
- Broken-resource replacement where your resource genuinely improves the destination.
- Paid sponsorships only for exposure or referrals, with appropriate link attributes.

Reject link packages, private blog networks, reciprocal-link schemes, automated guest posts, and contracts guaranteeing followed links or rankings.

## 8. Technical and trust checklist

The site could not be inspected, so every item is unverified:

- Confirm important pages return `200`, allow indexing, and use self-referencing canonicals.
- Exclude drafts, search results, filtered combinations, and thin location variants from indexation.
- Include only canonical, publishable URLs in the XML sitemap.
- Give every indexed location page distinct titles, H1s, descriptions, and visible body content.
- Add schema only for facts visible on the page; do not fabricate addresses, reviews, ratings, or offices.
- Maintain consistent business identity and contact details.
- Publish About, contact, privacy, terms, refund/cancellation, and relevant licensing information.
- Check mobile usability and performance using field data when available.
- Use hreflang only for genuine localized language/region versions.

## 9. 7/30/90-day execution

### Next 7 days

1. Supply the product URL or PRD, target country/language, real service areas, and conversion goal.
2. Choose one primary city based on operational importance—not guessed keyword volume.
3. Build one complete pilot location page using real proof.
4. Configure Search Console, analytics, conversion events, canonical tags, and sitemap inclusion.
5. Establish baseline indexation and branded/non-branded query reporting.

### By day 30

1. Inspect live results for the primary term and five long-tails.
2. Compare the winning page types, local-pack presence, dominant brands, and recurring wording.
3. Improve the pilot page based on observed intent.
4. Earn a small number of relevant citations or editorial mentions.
5. Publish up to three additional city pages only if each passes the publication gate.
6. Add contextual internal links from service pages, case studies, and the service-area hub.

### By day 90

1. Expand only pages gaining relevant non-brand impressions, enquiries, or useful query coverage.
2. Rewrite pages with impressions but poor clicks when the title or proposition mismatches intent.
3. Consolidate pages with overlapping queries or insufficient differentiation.
4. Remove or `noindex` thin pages that cannot be made useful.
5. Turn recurring customer questions into supporting guides and link them back to the conversion page.

## 10. Measurement and decision rules

Track weekly:

- Indexed versus submitted location pages.
- Non-brand impressions and clicks by page and query.
- Movement for the primary term and five mapped long-tails.
- Organic enquiry or signup conversion by landing page.
- Location-page duplication and query cannibalization.
- Referral traffic and conversions from legitimate citations.

After indexability is confirmed, use an 8–12 week test window:

- **Expand:** The pilot earns relevant non-brand impressions across several mapped queries and produces qualified engagement or a lead.
- **Improve:** It earns impressions but weak clicks or conversions.
- **Consolidate:** Multiple URLs receive impressions for the same primary intent.
- **Stop:** It receives no relevant impressions after technical issues are excluded, or the location lacks enough unique value to justify a page.

## 11. Avoid or defer

- Doorway pages that funnel visitors elsewhere.
- Hidden or off-screen keyword text.
- Bought followed links intended to manipulate ranking.
- Automated city/neighborhood permutations.
- Separate pages for spelling and “near me” variants.
- Fake offices, addresses, reviews, local staff, or service availability.
- Broad national head terms until the local pilot supplies evidence.
- Emergency, licensed, or same-day claims unless operationally true.
