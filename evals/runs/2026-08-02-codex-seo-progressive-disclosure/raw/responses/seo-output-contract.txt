# 90-day SEO plan: ownership-aware client onboarding for small agencies

**Market:** English, United States  
**Available capacity:** Six hours per week  
**Primary conversion:** Product signup, demo request, or waitlist submission—use whichever reflects the product’s actual availability  
**Authority:** New domain; no known backlinks or GSC history  
**90-day outcome:** An instrumented four-page cluster that tests whether small agencies search for an onboarding workflow—not a broad attempt to compete with established CRM and portal vendors.

## 1. Strategic decision

### Primary cluster

**Primary keyword:** `agency client onboarding workflow`

- **Intent:** Mixed commercial and informational
- **Target:** Homepage
- **Demand:** Unknown
- **Difficulty:** Unknown
- **Why this term:** It closely matches the product and audience while retaining the supplied “workflow” language. It is more specific than `client onboarding software` and creates room for the ownership differentiator.
- **Positioning:** A workflow in which every next step has a clear owner, rather than another static checklist.
- **Confidence:** Moderate on relevance; low on search demand and ranking potential because the supplied autocomplete and SERP notes were not independently verified.

The homepage should own commercial workflow intent. Supporting pages should answer narrower informational or template intent without trying to rank for the same primary query.

Internal-link rules:

- The homepage links to all three supporting resources.

## 6. Homepage brief

**URL:** `/`  
**Primary keyword:** `agency client onboarding workflow`  
**Secondary language:** `small agency client onboarding workflow`, `client onboarding workflow with task owners`  
**Intent:** Commercial evaluation with enough educational content to explain the category

**Suggested title:**  
`Agency Client Onboarding Workflow With Clear Ownership`

Append the brand name only if the title remains concise.

**Suggested H1:**  
`A client onboarding workflow where every next step has an owner`

**Meta description angle:**  
`Keep small-agency client onboarding moving with a workflow that makes the next step and its owner clear—from handoff through kickoff.`

Adjust this if the product does not cover every referenced stage.

### Recommended page structure

1. **Hero**
   - State the audience: small agencies.
   - State the problem: onboarding stalls when the next step has no clear owner.
   - Present one primary CTA.
   - Show a real workflow screenshot or product example when available.

2. **The failure of static checklists**
   - Explain that task lists describe work but do not establish responsibility.
   - Avoid claiming that all checklists fail.
   - Link to the checklist article.

3. **How the ownership-aware workflow works**
   - Show stages, next steps, and ownership using only real product capabilities.
   - Use a visual example with agency, client, and internal handoffs.
   - Do not imply automation, reminders, due dates, or integrations unless they exist.

4. **A representative agency onboarding flow**
   - Deal-to-delivery handoff
   - Required client information
   - Internal preparation
   - Kickoff readiness
   - Delivery-team handoff

5. **Who owns what**
   - Explain how the agency and client can tell who is expected to act next.
   - Link to the process guide.

6. **Checklist versus owned workflow**
   - Checklist: what should happen.
   - Workflow: what happens next and who owns it.
   - CRM/portal: describe only factual distinctions supported by the product.

7. **Template preview**
   - Give visitors a useful preview rather than gating all value.
   - Link to the template page.

8. **Trust and conversion**
   - Real screenshots, founder identity, contact route, product status, and transparent pricing link.
   - One repeated primary CTA.
   - No invented customer counts, testimonials, outcomes, or logos.

9. **FAQ**
   - What is an agency client onboarding workflow?
   - Who should own client onboarding?
   - What is the difference between a checklist and a workflow?
   - Can a small agency use this without a CRM?
   - How do you prevent onboarding tasks from being missed?

### CTA

Use one product-state-appropriate action consistently:

- `Start your onboarding workflow` if self-serve;
- `See the workflow` if a demo is required; or
- `Join the early-access list` if prelaunch.

Record it as one analytics event such as `organic_primary_cta`.

## 9. Measurement plan

### Setup

1. Verify the domain in Google Search Console.
2. Submit the sitemap.
3. Record index status for every cluster URL.
4. Install analytics with first-touch landing-page attribution.
5. Track:
   - Homepage primary CTA
   - Pricing visits from organic landing pages
   - Template use/download
   - Signup, waitlist, or demo completion
6. Annotate every publication and material title/content change.

### Weekly review: approximately 30 minutes

Track without assigning fabricated targets:

- Indexed versus submitted pages
- Non-brand impressions and clicks by landing page
- Queries containing `agency`, `client onboarding`, `workflow`, `checklist`, `process`, `owner`, and `template`
- CTR as a diagnostic, only after a page receives impressions
- Average position as directional—not a precise rank guarantee
- Organic CTA completions and downstream signup/demo/waitlist completions
- Unexpected queries that reveal a different category or intent

Manually check the primary term and five P0/P1 long-tails at launch and monthly using a consistent U.S.-oriented setup. Record result type and whether the site appears; do not treat personalized manual checks as authoritative ranking data.

### Decision gates

- **Day 30:** Confirm that published pages are crawlable, submitted, and either indexed or have a diagnosed indexing issue. Do not judge demand before this baseline exists.
- **Day 60:** If workflow and agency-modified queries generate impressions, retain the homepage focus. If only checklist queries appear, strengthen informational-to-product paths rather than forcing commercial claims.
- **Day 90:** Expand only the language producing relevant impressions or conversions. If the pages are indexed but the cluster produces no meaningful non-brand visibility, recheck actual U.S. SERPs and positioning before publishing more pages.
- If owner-specific language appears as query variants but not a separate intent, expand sections on existing pages. Do not immediately create an “ownership” doorway page.
- If broad software terms appear but produce irrelevant visits or no conversions, keep them deferred.

No numerical traffic or conversion target should be set until a baseline exists.

## 10. 30/60/90 backlog

### Weekly capacity allocation

Use the six hours approximately as follows:

- 3 hours: page creation or improvement
- 1 hour: product screenshots, examples, and on-page implementation
- 1 hour: technical checks and measurement
- 1 hour: distribution, relevant outreach, and query review

Do not exceed four cluster pages in the first 90 days.

### First seven days

1. Spend two hours finalizing the homepage’s audience, ownership promise, and single CTA.
2. Spend two hours implementing the homepage title, H1, core copy, and internal links.
3. Spend one hour setting up GSC, sitemap submission, and analytics events.
4. Spend one hour documenting the existing checklist URL, indexability, current title/H1, and expansion outline.

**Shippable result:** An ownership-focused homepage plus a functioning measurement baseline.

### By day 30

- Publish the rewritten homepage.
- Expand the existing checklist article into a useful agency-specific resource.
- Add reciprocal internal links between homepage, checklist, and pricing.
- Complete the technical baseline for current pages.
- Add real product imagery or a clearly labeled example workflow.
- Establish the first GSC and analytics baseline.
- Record title, publication date, target query, and CTA for each page.

### Days 31–60

- Publish the process guide focused on stages, roles, next-step ownership, and handoffs.
- Publish one genuinely usable workflow template page.
- Link all four pages according to the cluster map.
- Review GSC by landing page and query family.
- Adjust titles or introductions only when impressions show an intent mismatch; avoid reacting to daily position changes.
- Conduct limited white-hat distribution: show the useful guide or template to relevant agency contacts or answer genuine community questions where linking is appropriate.

### Days 61–90

- Refresh the two pages showing the clearest relevant impressions or product-intent visits.
- Add real examples, screenshots, or founder explanations where visitors need more proof.
- Improve weak snippets only on pages with enough impressions to diagnose CTR.
- Merge or redirect any accidental overlap instead of maintaining competing pages.
- Seek a small number of legitimate mentions through partnerships, expert contributions, or useful community participation; do not purchase links.
- Decide whether to continue, narrow, or reposition the cluster.
- Create an automation page only if both product capability and observed query/conversion evidence justify a distinct commercial intent.

## 13. Risks

- The proposed primary keyword may have little demand; relevance does not prove search opportunity.
- Searchers may understand the product as project management, a client portal, CRM, or automation software. GSC query language should guide later category decisions.
- “Ownership-aware” may convert well as positioning while attracting little direct search demand.
- A new domain may take time to be discovered and trusted even if pages are technically sound.
- The founder’s capacity makes page proliferation especially costly; maintain one intent per page.
- A template that is only a thin email gate will weaken trust. It must provide standalone value.
- Search visibility or conversions would still not prove payment, retention, or product-market fit.
