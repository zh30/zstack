## 1. Executive decision

Target one narrow commercial cluster:

- **Primary keyword:** `client onboarding workflow for agencies`

Agency workflow language appears more mixed, making the narrower audience-and-job formulation a more sensible test for a zero-authority domain.

## 5. Homepage brief

**URL:** `/`  
**Primary intent:** Evaluate a workflow product for an agency  
**Primary keyword:** `client onboarding workflow for agencies`

**Suggested title:**  
`Client Onboarding Workflow for Small Agencies | [Brand]`

**Suggested H1:**  
`Keep every client onboarding step owned and moving`

**Meta description:**  
`Give every client onboarding step a clear owner and next action. Build a repeatable workflow that helps your small agency keep new clients moving.`

### Recommended structure

1. **Hero**
   - State the audience, job, and ownership benefit immediately.
   - Primary CTA: the real product action, such as “Start a workflow” or “Request a demo.”
   - Secondary CTA: “See an example workflow.”

2. **The stalled-handoff problem**
   - Explain why ordinary checklists go unfinished: the next action exists, but responsibility is ambiguous.
   - Use the supplied community wording naturally rather than presenting it as research proof.

3. **How the workflow works**
   - Define the actual product mechanics: how an owner is assigned, how the next step is exposed, and what happens during a handoff.
   - Remove any mechanism the product does not genuinely support.

4. **Example agency workflow**
   - Show a real sequence such as contract, kickoff preparation, asset collection, access, strategy, and launch.
   - For each step, show owner, completion condition, and next action where supported.

5. **Ownership versus a static checklist**
   - Compare concepts, not competitors.
   - Explain that a checklist records tasks while an ownership-aware workflow also clarifies responsibility and handoffs.

6. **Automation**
   - Describe only implemented triggers, reminders, assignments, or integrations.
   - Do not imply full automation if the product merely organizes manual work.

7. **Small-agency use cases**
   - Founder-led agency
   - Account manager handing work to delivery
   - Client-dependent tasks and approvals

8. **Product proof and trust**
   - Current screenshots or a short annotated walkthrough
   - Founder/company identity
   - Security and privacy facts only where accurate
   - Link to pricing and product documentation

9. **FAQ**
   - What is a client onboarding workflow?
   - Who should own client onboarding?
   - How do agencies prevent onboarding checklists from stalling?
   - Can client onboarding handoffs be automated?
   - How is this different from a CRM or client portal?

10. **Final CTA**
    - Repeat the same primary action; do not introduce a competing newsletter CTA.

### Internal links

- Link to the checklist using descriptive text such as “agency client onboarding checklist.”
- Link to the process guide as “how to design your onboarding process.”
- Link to pricing near the first product explanation and final CTA.
- Supporting pages should link back with varied, natural anchors—not repeated exact-match text.

## 8. Measurement plan

Set up measurement before publishing so the first data is not lost.

### Weekly scorecard

- GSC impressions and clicks by page and query
- US-only non-brand query impressions
- Indexed status for each target URL
- Organic landing sessions, if analytics is available
- Homepage and article CTA clicks
- Signup, trial, or demo completions attributed loosely to an organic landing
- Queries containing `workflow`, `agency`, `checklist`, `process`, `owner`, `next step`, `automation`, or `template`

Keep brand and non-brand acquisition separate. A branded click does not validate the target cluster.

Manually check the primary keyword and five P0/P1 long-tails once a month from a consistent US setup. Treat reported positions as directional because personalization, location, and SERP features can change them.

### Decision rules

- **Technical correction:** If a target page is not indexed, resolve indexability, canonical, rendering, or content-quality issues before publishing more.
- **Improve the snippet:** If relevant impressions appear but clicks do not, compare query intent with the title and description before changing the page’s subject.
- **Improve conversion:** If relevant clicks arrive but product actions do not, inspect message match, product proof, CTA, and onboarding friction.
- **Expand:** Create one new page only when GSC exposes a distinct intent that the current pages cannot satisfy cleanly.
- **Narrow or stop:** If all target pages are indexable and crawlable but show no relevant non-brand impressions by day 90, pause expansion and reconsider the query wording, audience, and channel.
- **Do not set traffic or ranking forecasts** until a real baseline exists.

## 10. Explicit avoid list

Do not spend the first 90 days targeting:

- `client onboarding software` as a standalone head term
- `best CRM`, `CRM for agencies`, or CRM comparison terms
- `client portal software`
- `project management software`
- `workflow software` or generic `agency software`
- Generic employee/customer onboarding queries
- Separate pages for every word-order or industry variant
- A standalone ownership-aware category page before search demand appears
- A separate template page before template intent is visible
- “Best tools” articles without firsthand evaluation
- Competitor-alternative pages without factual, maintainable comparisons
- City or state doorway pages for a non-local product
- AI-generated article volume, programmatic synonym pages, or thin glossary posts
- Bought links, link exchanges at scale, private blog networks, hidden text, fake reviews, or unsupported review schema
- Claims such as “guaranteed to prevent missed steps” or invented performance percentages
