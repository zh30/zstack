## 1. Method and evidence limits

This sample is sufficient for pattern detection, not market sizing or a broad product green light.

Material limits:

- The summaries contain no verbatim quotes; none are manufactured below.
- Interview dates, company sizes, onboarding volumes, and recruitment source per participant are unknown.
- The warm interview cannot be identified, so possible relationship bias cannot be isolated.
- Question wording is unavailable. Leading questions, pitch leakage, and future-tense traps cannot be audited reliably.
- Several summaries omit the most recent incident, exact frequency, and monetary amount.
- The PM’s pilot request is stronger than a compliment, but it is not equivalent to starting, paying for, or completing a pilot.
- No evidence yet establishes retention, scalable acquisition, security acceptance, or willingness to migrate actual data.

### Mom Test audit

The evidence contains useful past behavior, especially for the PM, bookkeeper, and marketer’s previous switching. However, the pitch compliment and possibly the pilot request occurred after product discussion. Without the questions or transcript, pitch leakage cannot be scored.

For the next round, recover the missing evidence with:

- “Walk me through the last onboarding that required copying across tools.”
- “How often did this happen in the last four weeks?”
- “Who performed each step, and what did that labor cost?”
- “What happened when ownership was unclear?”
- “What have you tried to replace this workflow?”
- “Show me how migration, CSV export, and sensitive files are handled today.”
- “Who can approve a pilot and its budget?”

## 8. Decision

**Verdict: Advance**

Advance only to a tightly scoped, paid-or-deposit-backed concierge pilot—not a broad client-onboarding platform.

The recommended wedge is:

> Reduce recurring cross-tool onboarding coordination for service businesses while preserving explicit ownership and reversible data movement.

The first pilot should target I-01, the agency PM, because that participant combines measurable time cost, existing automation spend, a failed alternative, and a concrete request for a pilot.

Pilot scope:

- One onboarding workflow.
- Three real client-onboarding cases.
- Integrate with the existing tools rather than requiring full migration.
- Assign a named owner and status to every handoff.
- Support CSV export from the beginning.
- Avoid storing sensitive files in the first pilot unless security requirements are explicitly accepted.
- Measure manual time, failed or missed handoffs, setup effort, and willingness to pay.

Advance does **not** authorize a large build. If the PM will not schedule access to live cases, name the budget owner, or make a meaningful commitment, reshape around the bookkeeping failure case or park the generalized concept.

## 9. Concrete downstream handoff

For `z-landing-smoke`, test one narrow promise aimed at agency operations/PMs: reduce repetitive cross-tool onboarding work while making ownership visible. Do not claim security, universal integrations, or automatic migration.

For `z-write-prd`, scope only the pilot wedge:

- Workflow steps with explicit owner and status.
- Minimal integration or manual-assisted synchronization for the PM’s current tools.
- Activity trail sufficient to diagnose missed handoffs.
- CSV import/export.
- Time and failure measurement.

Non-goals:

- Full migration from every incumbent tool.
- A generic template marketplace.
- Autonomous handling of sensitive bookkeeping files.
- Broad SMB positioning.
- Building for founders who have no recent pain or spend.
- Enterprise-grade integration breadth before a pilot validates the workflow.

Safe copy inputs—paraphrases from summaries, not customer quotations:

- 45 minutes copying across tools.
- Unclear ownership caused a template to be abandoned.
- Weekly onboarding failures.
- Paying assistants or VAs to keep onboarding moving.
- Migration, CSV portability, and sensitive files affect switching.

Open questions for the next interviews:

- What is the last concrete onboarding failure?
- How many onboardings occur per month?
- What is the fully loaded monthly labor and tool cost?
- Which system is authoritative for each field?
- Who owns purchasing and implementation?
- What minimum migration path makes a trial acceptable?
- Which files are sensitive, and can the product avoid handling them?
- What event would cause the customer to abandon the pilot?
