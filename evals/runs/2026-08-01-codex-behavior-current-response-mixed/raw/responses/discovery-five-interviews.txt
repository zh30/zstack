# Discovery synthesis — client onboarding workflow

## 1. Method and evidence limits

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Completed | 5 interviews |
| Recruitment mix | 4 cold, 1 warm |
| Sample | Agency PM, consultant, founder, bookkeeper, marketer |
| Evidence source | User-provided summaries only |
| Evidence class | Primary interview behavior, but summarized and small-sample |
| Verbatim quotes | None supplied; no statements below are presented as quotes |

Limits:

- The five roles span different workflows and risk profiles, so this is pattern detection—not proof of one broad ICP.
- The warm interview was not identified, preventing interview-level bias adjustment.
- Without transcripts, question wording, leading questions, pitch leakage, chronology, and exact costs cannot be audited reliably.
- There is no observed product usage, payment commitment, retention, or completed migration.
- “Asked for a real pilot” is meaningful action-oriented interest, but remains weaker than actually starting one.

The agency PM is the strongest pilot candidate: concrete repeated labor, paid tooling, a failed alternative, an identified coordination failure, and a direct pilot request all occur in one account.

The bookkeeper confirms that recurring manual failure and paid labor are not unique to agencies, but sensitive-file requirements make bookkeeping a materially different security and compliance segment. It should not be bundled into the first wedge without further validation.

## 8. Decision

**Verdict: Advance**

Advance only into a tightly scoped concierge pilot and behavioral smoke test—not a broad product build.

The strongest supported wedge is:

> Agency project managers who repeatedly coordinate client onboarding across multiple tools, already pay for automation or manual labor, and still lose time because ownership and handoffs are unclear.

Why Advance rather than Reshape or Park:

- G1 and G2 have repeated behavioral support from independent interviews.
- I1 supplies an unusually actionable combination of pain, existing spend, failed workaround, switching objection, and a requested pilot.
- The uncertainty is primarily around switching and delivery, which a real pilot can test cheaply.
- The heterogeneous sample requires narrowing the ICP, but does not require abandoning the underlying problem.

Bookkeeping should remain a separate follow-up hypothesis because its sensitive-file constraint may produce a different product and sales motion. The founder segment is excluded unless future interviews show recent pain and current cost.

## 9. Concrete downstream handoff

### Immediate experiment: concierge agency-PM pilot

**Owner:** Founder  
**Recruit:** I1 agency PM  
**Duration:** 14 days or two real client onboardings, whichever comes first  
**Scope:** One existing onboarding workflow involving at least two tools  
**Delivery:** Manual or lightweight prototype; no generalized automation platform

The pilot should provide only:

- Explicit owner and due status for each onboarding item.
- A visible handoff trail across the existing tools.
- CSV import and export.
- Assisted setup using the client’s current workflow.
- Links or metadata rather than copied sensitive files where feasible.
- A weekly summary of manual steps avoided and exceptions encountered.

Before starting, obtain four concrete commitments:

1. A scheduled kickoff date.
2. Access to map one real workflow using redacted data if necessary.
3. Agreement to run two live onboardings through the pilot.
4. A decision meeting on a fixed date, including the budget owner.

Do not treat another verbal “yes” as validation.

### Success and failure thresholds

Advance from concierge pilot to a thin MVP only if:

- The PM completes two real onboardings through it.
- Manual copying falls by at least 50% from the reported 45-minute baseline.
- Every required handoff has an identifiable owner without maintaining a parallel template.
- The PM requests continued use and accepts a paid continuation, deposit, or signed paid-pilot agreement.
- CSV portability and migration assistance are sufficient to prevent abandonment.

Reshape if usage occurs but ownership visibility—not automation—is the main retained value. In that case, position the product as an onboarding handoff control layer rather than another automation tool.

Park this wedge if the PM does not expose a real workflow, reverts to existing tools during both onboardings, or refuses any concrete paid continuation after receiving measurable value.

### Next discovery round

Recruit five additional cold agency PMs matching the wedge. Require evidence of onboarding within the last 30 days and at least one of:

- Repeated cross-tool copying.
- Paid automation.
- Paid operational assistance.
- A failed template or automation attempt.

Ask each about the last onboarding before mentioning the product. Capture actual tools, elapsed time, frequency, failures, ownership, cost, migration history, security constraints, purchase authority, and the consequence of leaving the workflow unchanged.

### Downstream artifacts

For `z-landing-smoke`, test these evidence-backed concepts as paraphrases, not customer quotes:

- Stop repeatedly copying onboarding information across tools.
- Make every onboarding handoff and owner explicit.
- Keep CSV import and export.
- Start through assisted migration rather than rebuilding the workflow alone.
- Work with existing tools instead of demanding an immediate replacement.

For `z-write-prd`, keep these non-goals until further evidence exists:

- A generic onboarding platform for every professional-services role.
- Bookkeeping-specific file storage or compliance features.
- A complete Zapier replacement.
- AI-generated workflow automation.
- Enterprise administration.
- Building for users with no recent onboarding pain or existing cost.
