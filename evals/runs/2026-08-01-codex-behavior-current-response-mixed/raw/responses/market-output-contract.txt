## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem in user language:** Support agents repeatedly reconstruct the correct browser context when switching to or resuming work for a client.
- **Proposed solution:** A Chrome extension that restores a named, client-specific set of tabs and tab groups.
- **Narrow ICP:** Small support teams whose agents handle multiple client accounts in Chrome and repeatedly reopen the same client-specific tools.
- **Buyer hypothesis:** Support lead or operations manager.
- **User:** Front-line support agent.
- **Non-ICP initially:** Single-client support teams, individual tab-management enthusiasts, enterprises requiring managed-browser approval, and teams satisfied with bookmarks or Chrome saved groups.
- **Core outcome:** Start a client session with the right browser context in one operation.
- **Primary wedge:** Lead-defined, client-specific workspace templates for multi-client support teams, restored locally by agents.

## 6. Commercial value

**Verdict: Mixed.**

The plausible buyer is a support lead paying to reduce context-switching time and standardize client setup. The strongest value proposition is operational consistency, not generic tab convenience: an agent starts with the approved tools for the correct client, and a lead can update the template.

That value is not yet established. The reported loss is estimated rather than observed, and free substitutes may already solve enough of the job. The product must prove that it saves measurable time or prevents setup mistakes beyond what a bookmark folder or saved tab group provides.

## 7. Independent-developer fit

**Verdict: Conditional.**

A narrow local-first MVP fits four weeks: save a named workspace, restore tabs and groups, and import/export a lead-created template. Keep the first version away from credentials, cookies, page content, analytics, and real-time collaboration.

The risk increases sharply with “team workspaces.” Cloud synchronization introduces authentication, permissions, data handling, billing, conflict resolution, and support. Enterprise-managed Chrome environments add security review and extension allowlisting.

Solo-founder fit remains reasonable only if:

- The MVP is local-first with simple template sharing.
- A support lead can approve installation without a long procurement process.
- Customer acquisition begins with a reachable niche rather than the general Chrome Web Store.
- Spending is capped until payment behavior appears.

## 8. Short-term return: 30–90 days

**Verdict: Low odds of $500 MRR; plausible odds of learning or obtaining a small paid pilot.**

- **Success metric requested:** $500 MRR within 90 days.
- **More realistic first milestone:** Two paid team pilots followed by evidence that agents repeatedly use restored workspaces.
- **Best plausible path:** Sell directly to small multi-client support teams, deliver a local-first extension, and charge per seat with a team minimum.
- **Why odds are low:** There is no verified payment signal, no proven distribution channel, unknown admin approval, and a requirement for approximately 42–72 seats at the captured price range.
- **What would falsify the path:** Agents cannot show recent repeated reconstruction; native groups/bookmarks perform similarly in observed tests; leads decline even a small paid pilot; or admins cannot approve the extension.

### Conditional four-week allocation

- **Weeks 3–4:** Paid pilots, reliability fixes, and usage measurement.

## 9. Entry direction

### Primary wedge

- **Who:** Small support teams whose agents serve multiple client accounts in Chrome.
- **Job-to-be-done:** “When I start or switch client work, open the approved client context immediately without reconstructing it.”
- **Buyer-facing promise:** Reduce client setup time and make workspace configuration consistent across agents.
- **MVP shape:** Named workspace templates; one-click tab/group restoration; local storage; lead-to-agent template sharing through export/import; no credential or cookie transfer.
- **Initial pricing hypothesis:** $10/user/month with a $50 team minimum, explicitly treated as a test.
- **Where to find the first users:** The original community participants and support leads, followed by the buyer communities identified in the manual channel scan.
- **14-day gate:** At least two teams commit money, and at least four agents complete three real work sessions using the prototype instead of their previous setup method.

### Backup wedges—not current recommendations

1. Lead-authored onboarding workspace templates for newly hired support agents.
2. A personal, local-only client launcher for agents whose organizations prohibit cloud synchronization.

### Explicitly do not start with

- A general-purpose tab manager.
- Full Chrome-profile replacement.
- Real-time team synchronization.
- Enterprise administration, SSO, or audit logs.
- AI-generated workspaces.
- Storage or sharing of cookies, credentials, or client page contents.

## 10. Risks and open questions

- Are the 12 reports independent, recent, and drawn from the intended buyer population?
- Can agents demonstrate the problem from a recent shift?
- Does restoring tabs materially outperform saved groups and bookmark folders?
- Is the lost time really 10–20 minutes per shift when observed?
- Does the lead control a software budget?
- Will organizations permit the necessary Chrome permissions?
- Which workspace elements matter: URLs, order, groups, pinned state, windows, or login separation?
- Do client URLs contain sensitive identifiers that should not be synchronized?
- Does usage persist after initial setup?
- Will teams pay per seat, per workspace, or only a low flat fee?
- Can a solo founder reach enough teams to acquire 42–72 paid seats in 90 days?
