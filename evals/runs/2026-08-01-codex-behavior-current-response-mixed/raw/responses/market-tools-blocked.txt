## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem in user language:** “Preparing invoices from scattered notes and time records is tedious, and I sometimes delay billing or forget billable work.”
- **Proposed solution:** AI converts approved work records into invoice drafts, suggests descriptions, checks for omissions, and drafts reminders.
- **Narrow ICP:** Independent consultants who bill several clients monthly and currently reconstruct invoices from calendars, notes, email, or time logs.
- **Non-ICP initially:** Consumers, product businesses, large finance teams, healthcare, regulated accounting workflows, accounts-payable automation, and users already satisfied with automated billing.

### What would constitute real demand

- Prospects show recent examples of delayed or incorrect invoices.
- They already spend meaningful time or money solving the problem.
- They provide real billing artifacts for a test.
- At least one pays or deposits money before a polished product exists.
- Users repeat the workflow in a second billing cycle.

### Kill signals

- Invoice creation already takes the target user only a few minutes.
- The real bottleneck is client approval, cash flow, or collections—not invoice preparation.
- Users will not share billing data with an AI service.
- Existing accounting software solves the workflow adequately.
- Every customer requires different integrations, tax logic, and human support.

## 6. Commercial value

**Weak—not demonstrated.** The plausible economic outcome is faster billing with fewer missed line items, potentially improving cash collection. The likely buyer is the person doing the invoicing. However, no evidence currently shows that invoice preparation is painful enough, frequent enough, or poorly served enough to support another subscription. The critical question is whether the product recovers money, not merely saves a few minutes.

## 7. Independent-developer fit

**Conditional.**

A constrained MVP—upload work records, generate a draft, require human approval, and export it—could be built by one developer. The dangerous expansion is becoming an accounting platform with bank connections, tax rules, payment handling, many integrations, and correctness guarantees.

Support and liability increase when users expect financial accuracy. Keep source evidence visible, never invent line items, and require approval before anything is sent. Distribution is currently unproven; direct outreach to one profession is more realistic than competing broadly for accounting keywords.

## 8. Short-term return: 30–90 days

**Odds: Low.**

Define initial return as **one paid pilot and three users completing two real billing cycles**, not vanity sign-ups or hypothetical interest.

- **Best plausible path:** Sell a manual-assisted billing workflow to one narrow professional group before building integrations.
- **Why odds are low:** No verified pain, acquisition channel, payment intent, or retention evidence exists.
- **What would falsify the path:** After 15 qualified conversations, fewer than five show a recent painful example, fewer than three provide real artifacts, or nobody pays a small pilot fee.

## 9. Entry directions

### Primary wedge: evidence-to-invoice drafts for independent consultants

- **Who:** Consultants billing multiple clients from calendars, notes, email, or time records.
- **Job to be done:** Reconstruct completed work accurately and turn it into a client-ready invoice draft.
- **MVP:** Manual upload or paste; structured draft with citations back to source records; human approval; PDF/accounting-system export. No autonomous sending.
- **Where to find the first 10 users:** Existing professional network, consultant communities, bookkeeping partners, and direct outreach to a single consulting specialty.
- **14-day metric:** Ten qualified conversations, five real pain examples, three artifact-based trials, and at least one paid pilot.

### Backup wedges

1. Agency invoice-preparation assistant for reconciling project activity against retainers.
2. Bookkeeper copilot that converts client-supplied evidence into reviewable drafts.

### Explicitly do not start with

- “AI invoicing for every small business”
- Full accounting or tax automation
- Autonomous invoice sending
- Accounts-payable automation for enterprises
- Numerous accounting, banking, and email integrations before payment evidence

## 10. Risks and open questions

- Is invoice preparation the actual bottleneck, or are approvals and collections more painful?
- How often are billable items genuinely missed?
- Will customers permit AI processing of client and financial data?
- Is source-to-draft traceability enough to establish trust?
- Is this a standalone subscription or a feature users expect inside existing software?
- Can one narrow profession be reached repeatedly at acceptable cost?
- Will users return during the next billing cycle?

Success means three trials and at least one real payment or deposit. Failure means no artifact sharing or payment after ten qualified offers.
