## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem in user language:** “Preparing correct invoices and chasing late payments consumes time, and mistakes delay payment.”
- **Proposed solution:** An AI assistant that helps create, check, send, and follow up on invoices.
- **Narrow initial ICP:** US-based independent consultants and small agencies issuing 10–100 recurring client invoices per month without dedicated accounts-receivable staff.
- **Non-ICP:** Enterprises, healthcare, government billing, international tax-heavy invoicing, and companies needing autonomous bookkeeping or payment execution.

The product definition remains ambiguous. Invoice creation, document extraction, accounts-payable automation, bookkeeping, and collections are different markets with different buyers and risks.

## 6. Commercial value

**Weak on current evidence.** A commercial case would exist if a defined group routinely loses meaningful time or cash because invoicing and follow-up are mishandled—and will pay to improve that outcome. Neither the loss nor willingness to pay has been observed. “Businesses send invoices” proves category activity, not demand for another AI product.

## 7. Independent-developer fit

**Conditional.** A human-assisted MVP for one invoicing system and one narrow workflow is solo-buildable. A broad autonomous invoice assistant is not an attractive starting scope because it introduces integrations, sensitive financial data, auditability, exception handling, customer support, and potentially consequential errors.

Distribution is also unproven. The strongest initial channel may be direct outreach through bookkeepers, fractional CFOs, agency operators, or niche professional groups, but no channel evidence currently supports that assumption.

## 8. Short-term return

Define success as **one paid pilot within 30 days**, not recurring revenue at scale.

- **Best plausible path:** Manually deliver overdue-invoice follow-up for one narrow profession, with AI used behind the scenes.
- **Odds:** Low from current evidence because no reachable audience, payment behavior, or acute trigger has been demonstrated.
- **What falsifies it:** Ten qualified prospects reveal few recent late-payment incidents; nobody will share a redacted workflow; or nobody accepts even a small paid pilot after seeing a concrete sample.

## 9. Entry directions

### Primary wedge: overdue-invoice follow-up for small agencies

- **Who:** US agencies with 10–100 monthly invoices and no accounts-receivable employee.
- **Job-to-be-done:** Detect overdue invoices and prepare context-aware, approval-required follow-up messages.
- **MVP:** CSV upload or forwarded invoice data, aging view, draft reminders, approval log, and outcome tracking. Keep sending and payment actions human-approved.
- **Where to find the first ten users:** Direct outreach to agency owners and bookkeepers already serving agencies; this channel must be validated.
- **14-day metric:** At least 5 workflow conversations, 3 users providing redacted invoice examples, and 1 paid pilot.

### Backup wedges

1. Pre-send invoice error checking for one professional-services niche.
2. A white-label follow-up assistant for independent bookkeepers.

### Explicitly do not start with

- A universal “AI finance employee”
- Autonomous payments, bookkeeping entries, or collections
- Enterprise accounting integrations
- Every country, tax regime, and invoicing platform
- Building a full SaaS before obtaining workflow access or payment

## 10. Risks and open questions

- Which single invoicing task is frequent and painful enough to buy?
- Is the buyer the owner, bookkeeper, operations lead, or accountant?
- Does the target experience the problem weekly or only occasionally?
- What do customers already use, and why would they switch?
- Will users grant access to financial documents?
- What error rate and audit trail are acceptable?
- Is this a standalone subscription or merely a feature of incumbent software?
- Can a solo founder support integrations and financial-data security credibly?
