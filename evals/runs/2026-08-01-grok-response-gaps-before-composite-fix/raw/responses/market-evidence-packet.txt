## 2. Idea framing

- **Problem (user language):** Support agents keep rebuilding client context (tabs/workspace) when switching clients; it can happen daily and burn 10–20 minutes per shift (lead estimates).  
- **Proposed solution (as stated):** Chrome extension that restores a **client-specific tab workspace**.  
- **ICP (narrow):** Customer-support agents (and their leads) who juggle **many named clients/accounts** in Chrome, where context switch is frequent and time is measured.  
- **Non-ICP (who to ignore first):** Casual multi-tab users, personal bookmark fans, IT-locked enterprises that ban extensions, teams whose “workspace” is already the helpdesk UI, and “team collaboration suite” buyers before single-agent restore works.

## 6. Commercial value

**Mixed / weak-to-mixed.** There is a plausible **time-saving chore** for support agents, and leads already speak in minutes-per-shift language—which *could* map to seat pricing if a budget owner believes the time save. However, commercial value requires **payment for the outcome**, and notes explicitly state **nobody has paid for this exact outcome**. Free Chrome features and bookmark preference compress WTP. Admin blocks mean revenue may concentrate in teams that allow extensions and care enough to standardize—a smaller, harder-to-reach set. The $7–12/user/month figure is only an **adjacent, dated, unverified** anchor; it must not be treated as validated price for this extension.

---

## 7. Independent developer fit

- **Build scope for MVP:** Feasible solo: name workspace → save tabs → restore by client key; skip multi-user sync first.  
- **Support / ops:** Extension store review, Chrome API churn, “my tabs didn’t restore” support; manageable if ICP is small.  
- **Distribution:** Agents via communities/leads; **IT gate** is the hard part—not classic viral consumer growth.  
- **Regulation / liability / capital:** Low capital ($500); avoid claiming security/compliance theater; client data in tabs may raise privacy concerns—keep local-first if possible.  
- **Skills match:** Unknown; assume browser-extension competence is required for the four-week window.  
- **Verdict:** **Conditional Yes**—good shape for solo **if** wedge stays single-agent restore and you sell/pilot before building team workspaces.

## 8. Short-term return (30–90 days)

**Success metric used:** founder target **$500 MRR in 90 days** (also track first **paid pilot** and **3 agents using restore ≥5 days/week** as intermediate).

- **Best plausible path:** One support lead allows install → 5–10 agents use client-keyed restore on real tickets → lead pays a small monthly pilot (even below $7–12) → expand seats inside same org or 2–3 similar teams.  
- **Why odds are Low:** No exact-outcome payments; free substitutes; admin risk; $500 MRR ≈ many seats or higher price with zero conversion history; four weeks + $500 limits marketing and enterprise sales.  
- **What would falsify the path:** Agents say Chrome groups are “good enough”; leads won’t put a card down for a pilot; IT blocks after one ask; usage dies after novelty week.

## 9. If you go deeper — entry directions

### Primary wedge (recommended)

- **Who:** Support agents on multi-client queues whose lead already measures handle-time / shift efficiency, on machines **allowed** to install Chrome extensions.  
- **Job-to-be-done:** “When I open client X, restore the exact tab set I need without rebuilding from bookmarks.”  
- **MVP shape:** Local (or simple sync later) **client ID / name → tab set save/restore**; one-click restore; optional “last used for this client”; **no** team workspace v1.  
- **Where to find first 10 users:** The same community pool that produced the 12 notes; warm intros from the 3 leads who estimated time loss.  
- **Metric in 14 days:** ≥5 agents restore ≥1 client workspace on ≥3 workdays **and** ≥1 lead states a yes/no on paid pilot price.

### Backup wedges

1. **Lead-only “shift time audit”** + lightweight restore for their team (sell the time narrative, not features).  
2. **Solo power-user freelancers** who support multiple clients without corporate IT (easier install; weaker path to $500 MRR).

### Explicitly do **not** start with

- Full **team workspaces** (only 2 asks; multiplies product + sales complexity).  
- Competing feature-for-feature with Chrome tab groups.  
- Enterprise SSO/admin console before any paid pilot.  
- Broad “tab manager for everyone” positioning.

## 10. Risks & open questions

- Notes are **unverified**; n is small; leads’ 10–20 min may be inflated.  
- **Nobody paid** for this exact outcome—demand may be preference, not budget.  
- **Admin block** rate unknown beyond “two may block.”  
- Differentiation vs **saved groups/profiles** unproven in product terms.  
- Price band **$7–12** may not transfer; could be $0 willingness after free tools.  
- $500 MRR / 90 days may require **B2B multi-seat** sales skill you haven’t exercised yet.  
- Privacy of client-related URLs in saved workspaces.

## 11. Next 48 hours (exactly 3 actions)

1. **Write a 5-question Mom Test script** and message the **3 support leads** (time-loss estimators) plus any of the **12** you can reach: last time they rebuilt client context, what they used (groups/bookmarks), minutes lost, whether IT allows extensions, and whether they’d pay **$X/seat** for client-keyed restore (offer 2 price points, e.g. $5 and $10). Success: ≥5 replies in 48h.  
2. **Build only a clickable or local prototype brief** (no store ship required): list of client names → save current tabs → restore; show it in a 2-minute loom/script for agents—**do not** implement team workspaces. Success: one artifact you can put in front of 3 agents.  
3. **Run a kill check with 2 “bookmark preferrers” and any admin-adjacent contact:** ask what would make them **not** install; document extension-policy reality. Success: written go/no-go on installability for at least one target team.
