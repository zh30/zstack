## 1. Executive decision

**Primary cluster:** `client onboarding workflow for agencies`

**Primary page:** Homepage `/`

**Positioning wedge:** An agency onboarding workflow in which every next step has a named owner—not another passive checklist.

This cluster is preferable to broad terms such as `client onboarding software` because:

- It closely matches the product and ICP.
- The supplied notes indicate mixed results for agency-workflow and owner-specific searches.
- It connects commercial workflow intent with the community’s actual problem language: “Who owns the next step?”
- Broad software results reportedly favor established CRM and portal vendors, making them poor initial targets for a zero-authority domain.

The first 90 days should build one compact cluster: a commercial homepage supported by one checklist, one usable template, and one ownership guide.

## 2. Search context and assumptions

### Searcher

The likely searcher is an agency owner or operations lead trying to make onboarding repeatable across sales, account management, delivery, finance, and the client.

Their problem is not simply “I need a checklist.” It is:

- Tasks are documented but still not completed.
- Responsibility shifts between the agency and client.
- Nobody can see who owns the next step.
- Handoffs stall after the contract is signed.
- Automation cannot fix an undefined process.

Page ownership rules:

- The homepage owns **workflow**, **process**, product positioning, and commercial evaluation.
- The checklist article owns **steps** and **checklist** intent.
- The template page owns **template**, copy, download, and implementation intent.
- The ownership guide owns **roles**, **responsibilities**, “who owns,” and “why it stalls.”
- Automation stays a homepage subsection until observed query data warrants another URL.
- Pricing targets branded and transactional visitors, not `client onboarding software`.

## 5. Homepage brief

### Metadata and heading

- **Title pattern:** `Client Onboarding Workflow for Small Agencies | Brand`
- **H1:** `A client onboarding workflow where every next step has an owner`
- **Meta description:** `Run agency client onboarding with clear owners, handoffs, deadlines, and client responsibilities. Build a workflow your team can actually complete.`
- **Canonical:** Self-referencing canonical for `/`
- **Primary CTA:** `Build your onboarding workflow` or the closest truthful product action

Avoid claims such as “never miss a task again” unless product evidence supports them.

### Recommended page structure

1. **Hero: outcome and audience**
   - Identify small agencies explicitly.
   - Explain the ownership-aware difference in one sentence.
   - Show the product or a concrete workflow, not an abstract illustration.

2. **Why checklists stall**
   - A checklist says what should happen.
   - An operational workflow also says who acts, when, and what blocks the next step.
   - Use the community language naturally.

3. **How the workflow works**
   - Define the onboarding stages.
   - Assign an owner to each next step.
   - Separate agency-owned and client-owned actions.
   - Add deadlines, dependencies, status, and escalation.
   - Automate reminders only after ownership is clear.

4. **Example agency workflow**
   - Contract signed
   - Payment or billing setup
   - Intake request
   - Access and asset collection
   - Kickoff scheduling
   - Internal handoff
   - Delivery-ready confirmation

5. **Ownership visibility**
   - Show exactly how users answer:
     - What is next?
     - Who owns it?
     - What is blocking it?
     - What happens when it is late?

6. **Agency use cases**
   - Keep these as sections, not separate SEO pages during the first 90 days.
   - Examples may include marketing, design, or development agencies only when the product genuinely supports them.

7. **Template preview**
   - Link to the workflow template page.
   - Demonstrate useful fields: step, owner, due rule, dependency, completion condition.

8. **Checklist versus workflow**
   - Brief comparison.
   - Link to the checklist article for the complete step sequence.

9. **Trust**
   - Founder identity and product rationale.
   - Real screenshots or a short walkthrough.
   - Clear pricing and policies.
   - Customer evidence only when genuine.

10. **Final CTA**
    - Repeat the concrete action and expected outcome.

### Internal links

The homepage should link descriptively to:

- “agency client onboarding checklist”
- “client onboarding workflow template”
- “client onboarding roles and responsibilities”
- Pricing

Every supporting page should link back using natural variants of “client onboarding workflow for agencies,” not the identical anchor every time.

## 7. Technical baseline

Because the live site and code were not inspected, all current statuses are unknown. Establish this baseline before publishing more content:

- Verify all public pages return indexable `200` HTML without requiring client-side JavaScript to reveal the primary content.
- Choose one HTTPS hostname and redirect every alternative consistently.
- Give every indexable page a unique title, H1, meta description, and self-referencing canonical.
- Create an XML sitemap containing only canonical, indexable URLs.
- Add a valid `robots.txt` that references the sitemap and does not block marketing assets or content.
- Remove accidental `noindex` directives from production while keeping staging and previews excluded.
- Use permanent redirects if the checklist URL changes.
- Add breadcrumb navigation to supporting content.
- Ensure important pages are reachable through ordinary HTML links, not only JavaScript controls.
- Confirm mobile layout, readable typography, stable media dimensions, and reasonable page performance; do not assume a Core Web Vitals problem without measurement.
- Add `Organization` and applicable product structured data only with factual, visible properties.
- Add FAQ schema only when the exact questions and answers are visibly present.
- Include author/founder, contact, privacy, terms, and clear pricing information.
- English-US is the only market, so `hreflang` is unnecessary unless localized versions are introduced.
- Keep sitemap generation, canonical rules, and metadata centralized in the application rather than manually maintaining each deployment.

## 8. Measurement plan

### Setup during week one

1. Verify the canonical domain in Google Search Console.
2. Submit the sitemap.
3. Add privacy-appropriate analytics.
4. Track:
   - Primary CTA clicks
   - Signup or workflow-creation starts
   - Completed signups or workflow creations
   - Template-use or download actions
5. Preserve first landing page and available referrer/UTM data with conversions.
6. Create a simple weekly sheet by query, page, and conversion—not a complicated dashboard.

### Weekly review

Monitor:

- Indexed versus submitted pages
- Non-brand impressions and clicks
- Queries containing `agency`, `client onboarding`, `workflow`, `checklist`, `template`, `owner`, and `responsibilities`
- Which URL receives impressions for each intent
- CTA and signup behavior by organic landing page
- Branded versus non-brand traffic
- Crawl or canonical errors

Do not set traffic or ranking forecasts without a baseline.

### Decision logic

| Observed state | Response |
|---|---|
| Page is not indexed | Fix crawlability, canonical, rendering, or content-quality issues |
| Indexed but receives no relevant impressions | Recheck intent, title, terminology, and internal links |
| Relevant impressions rise but clicks do not | Improve title and description alignment; inspect actual queries |
| Clicks arrive but no meaningful product action occurs | Rework promise, proof, CTA, or product-message fit |
| Two URLs receive the same query family | Merge, redirect, or differentiate before publishing more |
| Template/ownership queries emerge | Expand the winning spoke with examples and supporting sections |
| Only broad software terms appear | Do not automatically pivot; evaluate relevance and SERP feasibility first |

At day 90, expansion should depend on observed GSC queries and product actions—not on the original keyword hypotheses alone.

## 9. Six-hours-per-week backlog

### First 7 days

- **2 hours:** Set up GSC, sitemap submission, and conversion events.
- **2 hours:** Rewrite homepage title, H1, hero, and ownership explanation.
- **1 hour:** Define canonical URLs and assign one intent to each page.
- **1 hour:** Outline the checklist rewrite and add initial internal links.

### Days 1–30: establish the hub

Expected capacity: roughly 24 founder hours.

- Complete the homepage rewrite and example workflow.
- Audit indexability, canonicalization, sitemap, robots, and server-rendered content.
- Rewrite the thin checklist article.
- Add contextual links among homepage, checklist, and pricing.
- Add truthful founder, product, privacy, and terms information.
- Record the first clean GSC and conversion baseline.
- Do not publish unrelated weekly blog posts.

### Days 31–60: ship the useful assets

- Publish the workflow-template page with a visible, usable template.
- Publish the ownership and responsibilities guide.
- Add template and ownership examples to the homepage.
- Link all four cluster pages bidirectionally.
- Review query-to-page alignment in GSC.
- Improve titles or snippets only where observed queries expose a mismatch.
- Seek a few legitimate mentions through founder relationships, agency communities, or partner resources—without buying or exchanging links.

### Days 61–90: consolidate evidence

- Refresh the page receiving the strongest relevant impressions with another worked example, clearer definitions, or better proof.
- Improve pages that get relevant impressions but weak engagement.
- Merge or differentiate any competing pages.
- Publish at most one additional article, chosen from actual GSC query language.
- Create a separate automation page only if relevant impressions and product capability justify it.
- Document which query family, landing page, and CTA produced the strongest signal.
- Choose the next quarter’s cluster from evidence; otherwise continue improving this one.

## 11. Risks and evidence handoff

- **Primary risk:** The chosen phrase may have little search demand; current evidence is directional only.
- **Competitive risk:** Search engines may interpret workflow queries as requests for established CRM or portal products.
- **Messaging risk:** Ownership language may resonate with users but not appear often as a typed query.
- **Operational risk:** Six hours per week is insufficient for broad content production and product work simultaneously.
- **Cannibalization risk:** Workflow, process, checklist, and template meanings overlap unless page ownership remains strict.
- **Conversion risk:** Search impressions would validate discoverability, not willingness to pay.

Current decision: execute the narrow cluster as a 90-day search experiment. Rankings, traffic, conversion, payment demand, and retention remain unproven.

The three actions this week are: rewrite the homepage around `client onboarding workflow for agencies`, establish GSC plus technical indexability, and turn the thin checklist into a genuinely operational agency resource. Avoid broad `software`, `CRM`, and `client portal` terms.
