# Market validation: AI invoice assistant

**Verdict: Demand is not proven.**

The underlying invoicing workflow is plausible, but with public web and social research unavailable, there is no primary evidence showing that a specific customer segment repeatedly experiences the problem, dislikes existing solutions, and will pay for this product. Treat the idea as a hypothesis—not a validated opportunity.

Assumptions: global SaaS for freelancers and small agencies; the assistant creates invoices, sends reminders, or helps collect overdue payments.

## Best hypothesis to test

Test an **invoice follow-up assistant for 2–20 person service agencies**, not a general-purpose AI invoicing platform.

- **Job:** identify overdue invoices and draft tactful, contextual follow-ups.
- **MVP:** accept an aging-report upload, produce editable reminder drafts, and track outcomes. Keep every message human-approved.
- **Success gate within 14 days:** at least 3 of 10 qualified prospects provide real invoice data, and at least 2 agree to pay or place a refundable deposit.
- **Kill signal:** prospects acknowledge the annoyance but refuse data access, already consider existing reminders sufficient, or will not pay.

Do not begin with autonomous payments, tax advice, bookkeeping, or deep integrations. Those increase trust, security, compliance, and support burden before demand is established.

## Key risks

- “AI” may be novelty rather than a purchasing reason.
- Financial-data access creates a significant trust barrier.
- Incorrect invoice details or overly aggressive reminders can damage customer relationships.
- Existing accounting products may already be good enough.
- A broad product would require numerous integrations and jurisdiction-specific behavior.
