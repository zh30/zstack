## 7. Checkpoint decision

**Verdict: Reshape**

Do not advance a generic “automated onboarding checklist for everyone,” and do not build a full client portal. Only two participants demonstrated strong, current pain, while the low-pain users are adequately served by free tools.

Do not park the opportunity either. I01 and I04 show repeated operational pain, concrete labor cost, failed outcomes, and existing spend. I01 additionally attempted an alternative and requested a real-client pilot.

### Reshaped hypothesis

Operations-heavy client-service teams need an ownership-aware onboarding runbook when one coordinator repeatedly moves work across several systems and already pays for automation or administrative help.

Initial wedge: **agency project managers coordinating client onboarding across existing tools**. I01 provides the cleanest combination of recent repetition, named workflow, direct software spend, failed workaround, ownership failure, and pilot pull.

Bookkeeping firms are an adjacent but separate security-sensitive segment. Do not merge their file-handling requirements into the initial thin pilot before the agency wedge is proven.

Anti-ICP:

- Solo operators who cannot recall a recent onboarding failure
- Users satisfied with a free checklist or automation
- Workflows taking roughly 20 minutes without meaningful failure consequences
- Prospects requiring a full portal or historical migration before testing

## 8. Concrete downstream handoff

### Recommended next artifact

A concierge-assisted thin pilot for I01, using one real client as requested. This should precede a broad PRD.

### Pilot promise

Turn one existing agency onboarding process into a reusable checklist in which every step has an owner, status, and automation trigger—without replacing the agency’s current systems.

### Thin scope

- Create one reusable onboarding workflow
- Assign an explicit owner to every step
- Track due state, completion, and exceptions
- Add only the minimum reminders or triggers needed for the pilot
- Link to existing Drive, Notion, email, and billing records rather than migrating historical data
- Export workflow status as CSV
- Record completion history sufficient to identify missed steps

### Non-goals

- Full client portal
- Historical migration
- Native sensitive-file storage
- Broad connector marketplace
- AI-generated onboarding processes
- Support for every solo-client workflow
- Bookkeeping-specific compliance features in the first pilot

### Behavioral success criteria

Advance from the reshaped hypothesis to a thin MVP only if:

1. I01 configures and runs the workflow for one real client.
2. The team completes that onboarding without maintaining a parallel ownership tracker.
3. Coordination time falls at least 30% from the observed 45-minute baseline, or a previously manual handoff is demonstrably removed.
4. After the first run, I01 starts a second client onboarding or accepts paid continuation.
5. At least one adoption condition—ownership clarity, low migration, or reuse—is confirmed through observed behavior rather than praise.

Test a $29/month continuation point because it matches the only explicit current software spend, while clearly treating price willingness as unvalidated.

Reshape again or park if the participant will not use it with a real client, still needs extensive migration, keeps the existing ownership tracker, or does not reuse it.

### Copy inputs for `z-landing-smoke`

No verbatim quotes were supplied. These are evidence-backed paraphrases, not customer quotations:

- Stop copying onboarding details between email, Notion, Drive, and billing.
- Give every onboarding step a clear owner.
- Pilot one real client without migrating everything.
- Keep sensitive client files in the systems you already trust.
- Export the complete checklist and status to CSV.

Suggested smoke-test headline:

> Reuse your client onboarding process without losing ownership across tools.

Suggested CTA:

> Run one real client onboarding

### PRD constraints for `z-write-prd`

Only write a production PRD after the live pilot clears the behavioral criteria. Carry forward these constraints:

- Ownership is a first-class field, not a text convention.
- Adoption must not require historical migration.
- CSV export is in scope.
- File handling and workflow orchestration are separate concerns.
- Sensitive-file storage is out of scope until its security requirements are validated.
- Compliments, portal browsing, and conditional pilot statements are not demand proof.

### Open questions for the pilot

- Is ownership clarity or cross-tool automation the primary value?
- Which one or two handoffs consume most of the observed 45 minutes?
- Can the workflow operate through links and metadata without copying client files?
- Who owns the software budget?
- Does reuse on a second client create enough value to replace or supplement the existing $29/month automation spend?
