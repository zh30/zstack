Client switching should not mean context rebuilding.

You search history for the admin page, ticket, docs, logs, and account record you had open yesterday.

A small workflow for a repetitive support job.

1Save a client workspace.Group the tabs you already use under that client.
2Keep contexts separate.Return to the right set instead of mixing accounts in one crowded window.
3Restore in one click.Reopen the saved workspace when that client needs you again.

Questions, answered plainly.

Is ContextDock available now?

No. It is an unbuilt concept being tested before development. Joining the waitlist does not promise access or a ship date.

What would it do?

The planned core job is simple: save the Chrome tabs used for a client and restore that workspace in one click. This test does not promise tab contents, logins, or unsaved work will be restored.

What will it cost?

Plans are currently expected to start from $9/month. Pricing may change before launch; joining the waitlist is free and creates no purchase obligation.

What about client data and privacy?

Privacy and workspace-storage design are not finalized because the extension is not built. We will not claim that tab data stays local or is encrypted until that architecture exists and is verified. The waitlist collects only your email and the bounded selections shown above.

What happens after I join?

Buttondown will handle confirmation. If the provider accepts your submission, check your inbox to confirm and receive occasional product updates. You can unsubscribe at any time.

Who is this not for?

It is not a help desk, CRM, password manager, session-recovery guarantee, or general-purpose bookmark organizer.
