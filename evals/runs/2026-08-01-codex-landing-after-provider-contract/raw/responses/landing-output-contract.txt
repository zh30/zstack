# 10-day smoke test: ClientTabs

“ClientTabs” is a working name only. Check domain, Chrome Web Store, and trademark availability before publishing.

Evidence limit: this package is based only on the supplied concept and constraints. Web access was unavailable, so no market demand, community rules, ad costs, or current Buttondown/Plausible API details were independently verified. The provider integration checkpoints below are launch blockers—not optional cleanup.

## 1. Smoke protocol

### ICP

Hands-on support agents and support leads at small US SaaS companies who repeatedly open different sets of tabs for each client account.

Exclude:

- General-purpose personal tab organizers
- Large enterprises requiring centralized device administration now
- Teams seeking ticketing, CRM, or knowledge-base software
- People who rarely switch between client-specific browser contexts

### Hypothesis

If hands-on support agents at small US SaaS teams are offered a Chrome extension that restores each client’s working tabs in one click, enough qualified visitors will join an early-access waitlist and describe a recurring client-switching problem to justify a thin prototype.

### Promise

Restore a client-specific tab workspace in one click.

This is a planned product outcome, not an existing capability.

### Offer

- Early-access waitlist
- Planned pricing from **$9/month**
- Early members help determine which tools and workflows are supported first
- No promised release date, discount, or permanent “founding” price

### Primary metric

Confirmed waitlist conversion rate:

`provider-accepted, non-duplicate subscribers ÷ qualified unique landing visitors`

A click or attempted submission is not a conversion.

### Secondary metrics

- Hero CTA click rate
- Form attempt rate
- Double-opt-in confirmation rate, if enabled
- Welcome-email replies containing a real workflow
- Explicit statements that the respondent would consider paying at least $9/month
- Paid cost per provider-accepted subscriber
- Bounce rate and engaged visit rate as diagnostics
- Invalid, duplicate, bot, and unattributed submission counts

Email opens are operational diagnostics only, not evidence of demand.

### Timebox

Ten consecutive days. Freeze the core page copy during a paid traffic batch. Correct broken tracking immediately, but record any interruption.

### Required samples and source-specific thresholds

Never combine warm outreach with cold traffic. Report every lane independently.

**Cold paid: Reddit ads**

- Target: at least 75 qualified unique visitors
- Strong: at least 6 accepted subscribers, at least 6% conversion, and no more than $25 paid cost per accepted subscriber
- Ambiguous: 3–5 accepted subscribers or 3–5.9% conversion
- Weak: fewer than 3 accepted subscribers and below 3% conversion after 75 qualified visitors
- If the $150 cap cannot produce 75 qualified visitors, mark paid acquisition inconclusive; do not increase the budget automatically

Both the minimum count and rate should be considered. A high rate from a tiny sample is not a pass.

**Cold organic: approved support/SaaS communities**

- Target: at least 50 qualified unique visitors
- Strong: at least 5 accepted subscribers and at least 8% conversion
- Ambiguous: 2–4 subscribers or 4–7.9% conversion
- Weak: zero or one subscriber and below 4% after 50 qualified visitors
- Traffic from different communities remains visible by source even if a combined cold-organic diagnostic is shown

**Warm outreach: known support practitioners**

- Send no more than 20 personal messages
- Target: at least 15 qualified unique visitors
- Strong warm signal: at least 4 subscribers, at least 20% conversion, and at least 3 substantive replies
- Weak: fewer than 2 subscribers after 15 visitors, with no specific problem replies
- Warm performance can improve copy and recruit interviews, but cannot by itself authorize building

### Contact-intent threshold

A substantive reply must mention at least one of:

- A repeated client-switching workflow
- A current workaround
- A concrete cost such as lost time, missed context, or opening the wrong account
- Tools that must be restored together
- Willingness or unwillingness to pay $9/month

“Cool idea,” emoji reactions, and email opens do not count.

# 5. Implementation package

## Architecture

- Host one static page on Cloudflare Pages.
- Submit the form to a same-origin Cloudflare Pages Function at `/api/waitlist`.
- The Function validates and normalizes the request, maps the exact UTM combination into an allow-listed lane, and sends the subscription to Buttondown using a server-held credential.
- The Function returns `accepted`, `duplicate`, `invalid`, `rate_limited`, or `provider_error`.
- The browser fires the Plausible conversion event only after `accepted`.
- Buttondown’s accepted subscriber record remains the conversion source of truth.
- Plausible supplies pageview, CTA, attempt, and attributed funnel diagnostics.

Do not expose the Buttondown credential in HTML or browser JavaScript.

## Suggested structure

- `/index.html`
- `/functions/api/waitlist.js`
- `/public/og-clienttabs.png`
- `/privacy/index.html`
- Cloudflare secret: `BUTTONDOWN_API_KEY`
- Configuration placeholders:
  - `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_ENDPOINT`
  - `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_AUTH_SCHEME`
  - `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_ACCEPTED_STATUS`
  - `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_DUPLICATE_SEMANTICS`
  - `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_METADATA_SCHEMA`
  - `VERIFY_PLAUSIBLE_SCRIPT_SRC`
  - `VERIFY_PLAUSIBLE_CUSTOM_EVENT_API`
  - `PLAUSIBLE_DOMAIN`
  - `CONTACT_EMAIL`
  - `PRIVACY_URL`

Because current official documentation could not be accessed, traffic must remain blocked until those mappings are checked against official Buttondown, Plausible, and Cloudflare documentation and dated in an implementation note.

## Form fields

Visible:

- `email`: required, email input, maximum 254 characters
- Honeypot field hidden from ordinary users

Hidden and populated from the landing URL:

- `utm_source`
- `utm_medium`
- `utm_campaign`
- `utm_content`
- `landing_variant`
- `traffic_lane`

Do not collect a name or free-text answer on the landing page. That keeps friction low and avoids storing support-related details through the form.

## Server validation

The Pages Function should:

1. Require `POST` and `application/json`.
2. Enforce an origin allowlist for the production and preview domains.
3. Reject empty or malformed emails.
4. Normalize the email only as supported by Buttondown; do not alter the local part based on assumptions.
5. Reject a filled honeypot.
6. Rate-limit repeated requests by a privacy-safe mechanism.
7. Match the submitted UTM tuple against the pre-registered allowlist.
8. Assign unknown or missing combinations to `unattributed`.
9. Submit the subscriber and attribution to Buttondown using its verified schema.
10. Treat only the documented accepted-new-subscriber response as `accepted`.
11. Treat duplicates separately and never increment conversion for them.
12. Return a bounded JSON result without echoing the email or provider response.

Do not log emails, free-text, credentials, authorization headers, or raw provider responses. Diagnostic logs may contain only a non-PII request identifier, bounded error category, and HTTP status.

## Opt-in and privacy

Use Buttondown double opt-in if its current configuration supports preserving source attribution through confirmation. This is appropriate for list quality and consent, though it will reduce confirmed-list volume.

Maintain two distinct metrics:

- `Waitlist Accepted`: Buttondown accepted the new subscription request
- `Email Confirmed`: the user completed double opt-in, if that status can be exported reliably

The privacy page must identify:

- The operator
- Buttondown as the email processor
- Plausible as the analytics service
- What data is collected
- Why it is collected
- Retention/deletion policy
- Contact method
- Unsubscribe and deletion process

Do not load additional ad pixels during this smoke test.

## Plausible events

Use these exact names after confirming the current Plausible event API:

- `Waitlist CTA Click`
  - Fire on a click that scrolls/focuses the waitlist form
  - Properties: `placement`, `variant`, `lane`

- `Waitlist Attempt`
  - Fire after browser validation passes and immediately before the request
  - Properties: `variant`, `lane`
  - Never report this as a signup

- `Waitlist Accepted`
  - Fire only after the Pages Function returns a verified provider-accepted, new subscriber result
  - Properties: `variant`, `lane`, `campaign`, `content`

- `Waitlist Duplicate`
  - Diagnostic only
  - Properties: `variant`, `lane`

- `Waitlist Error`
  - Properties: `category`, `lane`
  - Allowed categories: `invalid`, `rate_limited`, `provider_rejected`, `network`, `unknown`

Never send an email address, complete URL containing email data, or free-text answer to Plausible.

## Required preflight tests

Before any outreach or ad spending:

- Production page loads on mobile and desktop.
- Canonical URL and OG image resolve.
- Plausible excludes operator/test traffic where practical.
- One valid cold-paid test creates a provider record with the correct attribution.
- One valid warm test creates a separately attributed provider record.
- An invalid email does not create a record or conversion.
- A duplicate does not create a second conversion.
- An unknown UTM becomes `unattributed`.
- A provider failure shows an honest error, not success.
- Refreshing or adding `?status=success` cannot create success UI or an event.
- Confirmation and unsubscribe links work.
- No secrets or email addresses appear in source, network logs, or analytics.
- The provider documentation URL and verification date are recorded for every `VERIFY_*` item.

# 9. Decision rules

## Proceed to a thin prototype

Proceed only if at least one cold lane reaches its strong conversion threshold and cold respondents supply:

- At least three substantive problem replies, and
- At least two explicit statements that $9/month is reasonable or worth considering

Keep evidence separated by original source in the report. Build only enough to test saving and restoring a simple named tab set.

## Continue research without building

Do this when:

- A cold lane converts strongly but produces few substantive replies
- Warm demand is strong but cold lanes are weak or inconclusive
- People report the problem but their required workflow is materially different
- Pricing objections dominate otherwise credible interest

Run five problem interviews, grounded in recent behavior, before changing the product scope.

## Rewrite and retest

Rewrite the hero or narrow the ICP when:

- Cold visitors click the CTA but fewer than 3% become accepted subscribers
- Replies reveal a sharper repeated job, such as restoring tabs by ticket rather than by client
- The current workaround is consistently described in language absent from the page

Test one material message change at a time using new campaign/content identifiers.

## Park or reshape

Park this version when both cold lanes reach their minimum samples and remain weak, with fewer than three substantive cold replies in total.

Warm enthusiasm cannot reverse that outcome. It may justify interviews, but not product construction.

## Acquisition failure, not product failure

Mark distribution inconclusive when:

- The paid cap cannot generate the minimum qualified sample
- Community posts are not permitted or receive negligible qualified traffic
- Tracking or source attribution fails
- Provider acceptance cannot be verified

Fix or change distribution before drawing a demand conclusion.

## Paid-intent exception

An unsolicited request to pay, pre-order, or receive an invoice is a high-value lead. Interview that person promptly and test a paid prototype offer later. One request is not proof of a scalable market.
