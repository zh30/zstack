No—the pooled 6% is encouraging, but it is not enough evidence to build the full product.

The core problem is attribution: you know the 12 emails came from 200 total visits, but not how many came from friends versus ads. The cold-ad conversion rate could therefore be anywhere from:

- 0% if all 12 signups came from friends.
- 6.7% if all 12 came from ads.

That difference changes the decision completely. Broad cold ads typically produce lower conversion than warm traffic, so pooling them hides the signal.

Recommended decision:

1. Do not build the full product yet.
2. Contact all 12 subscribers with a concrete use-case question and a price or deposit ask. Replies, calls, and payments are stronger evidence than email capture.
3. Run another test with source attribution preserved and at least 200 qualified cold visitors—not merely broad traffic.
4. Pre-register the cold threshold:

   - Continue to a thin MVP: at least 5% confirmed email conversion plus several substantive replies, or any credible payment intent.
   - Revise and retest: 2–5% with weak engagement.
   - Reshape the offer or ICP: below 2% after 200+ qualified visits across two controlled variants.

If you discover that most of the 12 emails came from ads, that supports building a small paid-value slice. It still does not justify the full product because email signup demonstrates curiosity, not sustained usage or willingness to pay.

Evidence is limited to your reported aggregate counts; individual signup attribution, traffic quality, confirmation status, replies, and payment intent were not supplied or independently verified.
