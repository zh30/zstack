## 1. Executive decision

Use **“agency client onboarding workflow”** as the primary cluster and assign it to the homepage.

This is preferable to broad terms such as “agency software” or “client onboarding software” because:

- It closely matches the product’s job-to-be-done.
- The supplied notes describe mixed results for agency-workflow and owner-specific searches.
- It accommodates the differentiator—making the next owner explicit—without depending on an unproven phrase such as “ownership-aware onboarding.”
- Broad software SERPs reportedly favor established CRM and client-portal vendors, making them poor first targets for a domain with no authority.

Confidence is **moderate on relevance, low on verified demand and difficulty**. The first 90 days should be treated as a focused search experiment, not a traffic forecast.

Do not create separate pages for every variation of “checklist,” “process,” “workflow,” “template,” and “automation.” Each page below owns a distinct intent.

## 2. Searcher and positioning

| Searcher | Job-to-be-done | Search mindset |
|---|---|---|
| Small-agency founder | Make onboarding repeatable without personally chasing every task | Commercial investigation |
| Operations or account lead | Clarify who must act next across the agency and client | Problem-aware, mixed intent |
| Agency owner building a process | Find a usable starting workflow or template | Informational moving toward commercial |
| Team using static checklists | Diagnose why tasks stall despite having a checklist | Informational |

Recommended positioning:

> A client onboarding workflow for small agencies that assigns every next step to an owner—inside the agency or on the client side—so work does not stall after the checklist is created.

“Ownership-aware” should initially be a differentiating concept in copy, not the primary keyword. Search data can later show whether people adopt or search that phrase.

## 5. Homepage brief

### Search and metadata

- **Primary keyword:** agency client onboarding workflow
- **Suggested title:** `Agency Client Onboarding Workflow | [Brand]`
- **Suggested H1:** `A client onboarding workflow where every next step has an owner`
- **Meta description:** `Run agency client onboarding without stalled handoffs. Assign every agency and client task to an owner, see what is next, and keep delivery moving.`
- **Canonical:** Self-referencing canonical for `/`
- **Primary CTA:** Start a workflow / Try the workflow
- **Secondary CTA:** View the onboarding template

### Recommended page structure

1. **Hero: category plus differentiated outcome**
   - Say “agency client onboarding workflow” naturally.
   - Explain that every next step has a named agency or client owner.
   - Show the actual product or workflow, not a generic team photograph.

2. **The failure mode**
   - A checklist can describe what should happen without making anyone responsible.
   - Work stalls at sales handoff, asset collection, approval, kickoff, or access requests.
   - Avoid unsupported performance claims.

3. **How the workflow works**
   - Create or start from a workflow.
   - Assign each next step to the agency or client.
   - Surface blocked and overdue handoffs.
   - Move the account into delivery with a clear record.

4. **Ownership model**
   - Explain task owner, approver, due date, dependency, and current blocker.
   - Include a small example involving an agency owner, account manager, and client stakeholder.

5. **Checklist versus ownership-aware workflow**
   - Checklist: records tasks.
   - Workflow: records sequence, state, dependencies, and ownership.
   - Do not attack checklists categorically; explain where they stop being sufficient.

6. **Use cases**
   - Website projects
   - Paid-media retainers
   - Creative or branding engagements
   - Recurring service onboarding

7. **Template preview**
   - Show several real steps and ownership fields.
   - Link to the dedicated workflow-template page.

8. **Trust**
   - Founder identity and product rationale.
   - Honest product status.
   - Screenshots or a short demonstration.
   - Customer proof only when it exists.

9. **FAQ**
   - Who owns client onboarding in a small agency?
   - What is the difference between a checklist and a workflow?
   - Can client-side tasks have owners?
   - How do we hand an account from sales to delivery?
   - Can the workflow use our existing onboarding process?

10. **Final CTA**
    - Repeat the concrete outcome: give every next step an owner.

### Internal links

The homepage should link to:

- Pricing with descriptive text such as “See onboarding workflow pricing.”
- The checklist article from the checklist-versus-workflow section.
- The template from the template preview.
- The process guide from the how-it-works section.

Every supporting page should link back to the homepage using varied, natural anchors—not repeated exact-match anchor text.

## 8. Measurement plan

### Establish during week one

1. Verify GSC and submit the sitemap.
2. Add privacy-conscious analytics.
3. Define one primary conversion event and one micro-conversion:
   - Primary: completed signup, waitlist submission, or request-access submission.
   - Micro: template copy/download or product-demo CTA click.
4. Annotate publication and major revision dates.
5. Record a zero-state baseline: indexed pages, organic sessions, impressions, clicks, and conversions. Zero is acceptable for a new domain.

### Weekly 20-minute review

| Signal | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Indexed status | Can Google discover and retain the page? |
| Impressions by page and query | Is the intended cluster receiving any visibility? |
| Query-to-page match | Is the correct page appearing for each intent? |
| Clicks and CTR | Does the snippet earn interest once shown? |
| Organic CTA and signup events | Does the traffic move toward product use? |
| Branded versus non-branded queries | Is acquisition extending beyond founder-generated awareness? |
| Referring domains | Is genuine distribution producing mentions or links? |

Do not set traffic or ranking forecasts without a baseline.

### Decision rules

- **Not indexed:** fix technical discovery, canonical, rendering, or quality issues before publishing more pages.
- **Indexed but no relevant impressions:** reassess query wording and search intent; do not solve it with bulk content.
- **Relevant impressions but weak clicks:** revise title and description to better express the ownership benefit.
- **Clicks but no micro-conversions:** improve product clarity, proof, template usefulness, and CTA alignment.
- **One supporting topic attracts qualified impressions:** deepen that page and its internal links before opening a new cluster.
- **Two pages surface for the same intent:** consolidate or differentiate them.
- **Only broad CRM/portal queries appear:** exclude those terms from page targeting and reinforce the agency-workflow wedge.

At day 90, compare the cluster against its own first observed 30-day baseline. Rankings, traffic, conversions, payment demand, and retention are all currently unproven.

## 9. 30/60/90 backlog

The six-hour weekly limit allows roughly one substantial page or improvement every two weeks.

### Days 1–30: establish the cluster

Budget: approximately 24 hours.

- **6 hours:** Install the technical and measurement baseline; verify indexability, sitemap, canonicals, GSC, and conversion events.
- **8 hours:** Rewrite the homepage around the primary workflow cluster and ownership positioning.
- **7 hours:** Expand the checklist article into a genuinely usable agency resource.
- **3 hours:** Add internal links, update pricing-page context, submit URLs for indexing, and record the baseline.

Outcome: one coherent commercial page, one strong informational page, and reliable measurement.

### Days 31–60: supply the reusable asset

Budget: approximately 24 hours.

- **10 hours:** Create the workflow-template page and copyable/downloadable artifact.
- **4 hours:** Add a real product screenshot, example workflow, and founder trust material to the homepage.
- **6 hours:** Review initial GSC query-to-page matching and refine titles, headings, and internal links.
- **4 hours:** Distribute the template through founder-controlled channels and relevant relationships. Seek editorially earned mentions; do not mass-post links.

Outcome: the cluster has a link-worthy asset and initial query evidence.

### Days 61–90: deepen only what earns evidence

Budget: approximately 24 hours.

- **10 hours:** Publish the process guide covering ownership and sales-to-delivery handoffs.
- **4 hours:** Improve the page showing the strongest relevant impressions or conversions.
- **3 hours:** Resolve cannibalization, indexing, or snippet problems identified in GSC.
- **3 hours:** Add FAQ content only from real prospects, users, or support conversations.
- **4 hours:** Run the 90-day review and choose one action:
  - deepen the workflow cluster;
  - test the automation spoke;
  - revise the category positioning;
  - pause SEO expansion if search fit is weak.

Outcome: a measured decision about whether this cluster deserves the next quarter.

## 11. Main risks

- The primary phrase may have little demand; relevance does not prove volume.
- “Workflow” searches may still resolve toward broad project-management or CRM tools.
- The product’s ownership concept may resonate in community discussion without being directly searched.
- New-domain discovery may be slow without genuine distribution and links.
- A static template could attract visitors who never need software.
- Six hours per week makes focus essential: three excellent interconnected resources are more realistic than a conventional content calendar.
- Search opportunity does not establish willingness to pay, activation, or retention.
