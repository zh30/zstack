# PRD: Ownership-Aware Client Onboarding Runbook

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | Draft for build |
| Owner | Solo developer |
| Last updated | 2026-08-02 |
| Version | 0.1 |
| Primary persona | Owner or operations lead at a small agency |
| Platform | Responsive web application; desktop-first |
| Delivery constraint | Six weeks, one developer |

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 One-liner

A lightweight web runbook for small agencies to reuse client-onboarding steps, assign an explicit owner and due date to each step, and preserve what was completed, excepted, or missed without becoming a client portal, file store, or bookkeeping system.

### 1.2 Problem

Small agencies often coordinate onboarding through spreadsheets, documents, chat, and external systems. The checklist exists, but responsibility and execution history are fragmented:

- Steps can be reusable without making ownership explicit.
- A task may be late without anyone noticing when it first became overdue.
- Exceptions get discussed in chat but are not preserved with the run.
- External client records are difficult to connect to the relevant step.
- Sensitive documents and financial records require systems outside this product.

The MVP must answer four questions for one live onboarding run:

1. What must happen?
2. Who owns each step?
3. What is due or overdue?
4. What happened, including exceptions and missed deadlines?

### 1.3 Evidence treatment

| Input | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Cold smoke traffic: 27 signups from 300 visits | Observed experiment: 9% cold-visit signup conversion. This supports interest in the proposition, not product use, retention, or payment. |
| Two pilot volunteers | Observed experiment: a concrete route to usability testing. Volunteering does not establish activation or willingness to pay. |
| Warm traffic: 12 signups from 40 visits | Observed experiment: 30% warm conversion. It must be reported separately because prior familiarity or trust makes it incomparable with cold acquisition. |
| Discovery supports owners, reusable steps, links, and CSV | Directional input used to shape P0. The discovery method, sample, and observed behaviors were not supplied, so feature demand remains insufficiently evidenced and must be verified in pilots. |
| Sensitive file storage and bookkeeping are separate | Product boundary supplied by the user; treated as a constraint, not evidence of demand. |
| $29/month | Assumption only. No payment or payment commitment was requested. |

No blended “39 of 340” conversion rate will be used for product decisions.

### 1.4 Product and business goals

Product goals:

- Let an agency define one reusable onboarding workflow.
- Let it execute one active client run with explicit step ownership and due states.
- Preserve completion, exception, reminder, and missed-step history.
- Reference external systems through links and export operational data as CSV.

Business goals:

- Determine whether both pilot volunteers can run real onboarding work without founder-operated workarounds.
- Measure repeat use after the first run.
- Test willingness to pay separately after users experience the workflow.

### 1.5 MVP outcome

A pilot user creates a reusable workflow, starts a live client run, manages every step to completion or a documented exception, and can later reconstruct which steps were missed and who owned them.

### 1.6 Non-goals

The six-week MVP will not include:

- Client portals or client accounts.
- File uploads, document storage, previews, or attachments.
- Bookkeeping, invoices, payment collection, or financial records.
- Spreadsheet or legacy-data migration.
- CRM, accounting, storage, calendar, email, Slack, or other connectors.
- AI generation, summaries, or recommendations.
- Native mobile applications.
- Enterprise roles, approval chains, SSO, or granular permissions.
- Compliance certification or a regulated-industry compliance claim.
- Automatic reminder delivery.

## 2. Users and product shape

### 2.1 Primary persona

| Attribute | Definition |
|---|---|
| User | Agency owner or operations lead at an agency small enough for one person to oversee onboarding |
| Primary job | Make every onboarding step explicitly owned, time-bound, and auditable |
| Current alternatives | Spreadsheets, shared documents, project tools, chat, and links to specialist systems |
| Trigger | A new client is signed and onboarding must begin |
| Main risk | A required step has no clear owner, is forgotten, or becomes late without a durable record |
| Anti-persona | Enterprises needing role hierarchies, regulated workflows, or customer-facing portals |

Secondary participants are represented as owner labels in the MVP. They do not receive product accounts or permissions.

### 2.2 Assumptions

- The agency operator can enter owners as names or labels without inviting those people.
- A date-level due state is sufficient; time-of-day scheduling is unnecessary.
- CSV means export, not import, because migration is excluded.
- Manual reminders mean recording an out-of-product reminder action, not sending an automated message.
- One workflow definition and at most one active run per workspace are sufficient for the pilot. Completed runs remain in history, and the workflow can be reused sequentially.
- Authentication can use a managed service; the PRD does not mandate a technical stack.

### 2.3 Value proposition

Unlike a generic checklist, the product makes ownership, due state, exceptions, and missed deadlines first-class parts of every onboarding step. Unlike an all-in-one client platform, it leaves documents, bookkeeping, and communication in their existing systems and links to those records.

### 2.4 Product principles

- Every actionable step has one explicit owner.
- History is append-only from the user’s perspective.
- External systems remain the source of truth for files and financial data.
- Manual control comes before automation.
- One reliable workflow and active run come before breadth.

## 3. Scope

| In scope: P0 | Out of scope |

| CSV export | CSV import or migration |

## 4. Journeys and states

### Journey A: Create the reusable workflow

1. The operator creates the workspace’s workflow.
2. They add, rename, reorder, and remove steps.
3. Each step receives a default owner label and optional due-day offset.
4. They save the workflow.
5. The saved definition becomes the source for a new run.

Success: an ordered workflow exists with at least one valid step.

Recovery: validation identifies missing titles or owners without discarding entered data.

### Journey B: Start and execute a live run

1. The operator enters a client/run name and start date.
2. The system copies workflow steps into a run snapshot.
3. It resolves each due date from the start date and due-day offset.
4. The operator adjusts run-specific owners or due dates if necessary.
5. They mark steps complete or record an exception.
6. The run closes when every step is complete or excepted.

Success: there are no unresolved steps, and the run remains available as read-only history.

Recovery: closing is blocked while any step is unresolved, with those steps identified.

### Journey C: Find and handle a missed step

1. The operator opens the active run.
2. An incomplete step passes its due date.
3. The system changes its current state to overdue and creates one missed-step history event.
4. The operator records a manual reminder, changes the due date, completes the step, or records an exception.
5. The original missed event remains visible and appears in CSV export.

Success: the current state can change without erasing the fact that the deadline was missed.

### Journey D: Reference and export records

1. The operator adds an external record link to a run or step.
2. The product stores the label and URL, not the linked content.
3. The operator opens the record in a new browser context.
4. They export the run to CSV.
5. The CSV contains the current step data and relevant historical outcomes.

Success: operational data can be reviewed outside the product without storing sensitive files.

### State model

Run states:

`Draft → Active → Closed`

A closed run cannot be reopened in P0. A new run may then be created from the reusable workflow.

Step resolution states:

- `Open`: unresolved, with no exception or completion.
- `Completed`: resolved with completion timestamp.
- `Exception`: resolved with required reason.

Due states, calculated independently from resolution:

- `Unscheduled`: no due date.
- `Upcoming`: due after the workspace’s current date.
- `Due today`: due on the current date.
- `Overdue`: due before the current date while still open.
- `Resolved`: completed or excepted.

Changing a due date updates the current due state but does not delete existing missed-step events.

## 5. Functional requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---:|---|
| FR-001 | The operator can create and maintain one ordered reusable workflow. | P0 | A workflow requires a name and at least one step; steps can be added, renamed, reordered, and removed before saving; every saved step has a non-empty title and owner label. |
| FR-002 | A workflow step can define a default owner and optional due-day offset. | P0 | Owner is required; offset accepts zero or a non-negative whole number; invalid values block saving with an inline error. |
| FR-003 | The operator can start one active run from the workflow. | P0 | Starting a run requires a unique run/client name and start date; workflow steps are copied as a snapshot; due dates equal start date plus offset; starting another run is blocked while one remains active. |
| FR-004 | Run-step ownership and due dates can be adjusted without changing the reusable workflow. | P0 | Edits affect only the active run; every run step retains exactly one non-empty owner; the UI displays the derived due state using the workspace timezone. |
| FR-005 | The operator can complete a step. | P0 | Completing an open step records actor, timestamp, and optional note; repeated submission does not create duplicate completion events; the workflow definition is unchanged. |
| FR-006 | The operator can resolve a step as an exception. | P0 | Exception requires a reason of 1–500 characters and records actor and timestamp; an excepted step counts as resolved but remains visibly distinct from completion. |
| FR-007 | The system preserves missed-step history. | P0 | When an open scheduled step first becomes overdue, exactly one missed event is recorded for that due-date occurrence; completing, excepting, or rescheduling does not erase it; a later newly missed due date can create another distinct event. |
| FR-008 | The operator can record a manual reminder. | P0 | A reminder record contains step, timestamp, actor, and optional note; the product makes no claim that a message was delivered; the record appears in step history. |
| FR-009 | The operator can attach external record links to a run or step. | P0 | A link requires a label and valid HTTP/HTTPS URL; no upload control exists; opening a link does not expose the current product page to the destination via an unsafe opener context. |
| FR-010 | The operator can close and reuse a runbook. | P0 | Close is enabled only when all steps are completed or excepted; the closed run remains viewable; closing allows a new run to be created from the current workflow. |
| FR-011 | The operator can export the active or closed run as CSV. | P0 | Export includes run identity, step order/title, owner, due date/state, resolution, timestamps, exception reason, external link URLs, reminder count, and missed-event count/dates; UTF-8 output opens correctly in common spreadsheet software; formula-leading user values are escaped. |
| FR-012 | The operator can inspect chronological step history. | P0 | History shows creation, ownership/due-date changes, reminders, missed events, completion, and exceptions with timestamp and actor; no UI action can edit or delete individual history entries. |

## 6. Non-functional and data-boundary requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance criteria or validation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| NFR-001 | Access requires authentication. | P0 | An unauthenticated request to any workspace, run, link, history, or export route is rejected or redirected; users cannot retrieve another workspace by changing an identifier. |
| NFR-002 | The MVP must not store files or linked-page contents. | P0 | Code and UI review confirms there is no upload endpoint/control, binary-object field, remote-content fetcher, or linked-page cache; only link labels and URLs are persisted. |
| NFR-003 | The MVP must not collect bookkeeping data. | P0 | No invoice, payment, bank, tax, ledger, or transaction entities or fields exist; CSV contains only runbook data. |
| NFR-004 | Core pages must remain usable on common laptop widths. | P0 | Manual validation at 1280×720 completes all P0 journeys without horizontal page scrolling; keyboard-only operation can reach and activate all controls. |
| NFR-005 | Ordinary interactions must provide visible feedback. | P0 | On a seeded run of 100 steps, save, resolve, filter, and history-open actions acknowledge input within one second under normal test conditions; longer operations show a progress state and prevent duplicate submission. |
| NFR-006 | Destructive changes require confirmation. | P0 | Removing a workflow step or discarding unsaved run edits requires explicit confirmation; closed-run history has no delete control in P0. |

## 7. Information architecture and critical states

Pages:

- Sign in
- Workflow editor
- New run
- Active-run overview
- Step detail and history
- Closed-run history
- Workspace settings: name and timezone

Critical states:

- Empty workflow: explain that a reusable step list is required; CTA “Create workflow.”
- No active run: CTA “Start client run.”
- Overdue step: show owner, due date, missed count, and actions without relying on color alone.
- Invalid external link: retain the entered value and explain accepted schemes.
- Export failure: keep the run unchanged and offer retry.
- Concurrent edit: reject stale updates and ask the operator to refresh rather than silently overwrite.
- Closed run: read-only presentation with export available.

Copy must say “Record reminder” rather than “Send reminder” and “External link” rather than “Attachment.”

## 8. Data boundaries

### 8.1 Core entities

| Entity | Purpose | Conceptual fields |
|---|---|---|
| Workspace | Isolation and date behavior | ID, name, timezone |
| User | Authenticated operator | ID, workspace ID, display name |
| Workflow | Reusable definition | ID, name, version/update timestamp |
| Workflow step | Reusable ordered instruction | ID, title, order, default owner label, due-day offset |
| Run | Client-specific execution | ID, client/run name, start date, state, workflow snapshot version |
| Run step | Executable snapshot | ID, title, order, owner, due date, resolution state |
| External link | Pointer only | Parent type/ID, label, HTTP/HTTPS URL |
| History event | Append-only operational record | Type, actor, timestamp, old/new values or reason |

### 8.2 Boundary rules

- Store no uploaded content, credentials for external systems, or copies/previews of linked records.
- Users remain responsible for access control in the linked system.
- Avoid free-form client data beyond run name, notes, exception reasons, and URLs.
- CSV export is initiated manually; there is no scheduled delivery.
- No third-party operational connectors are required for MVP.
- Retention and account deletion policy are open decisions before public launch; pilots must receive a plain-language statement of current handling.

## 9. Metrics and decision triggers

### 9.1 Product validation metrics

| Metric | Definition | Six-week signal |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot activation | Volunteer creates a workflow and starts a real run without developer data entry | Both pilots activate |
| Ownership coverage | Active-run steps with a non-empty explicit owner | 100%, enforced by product |
| Run resolution | Started pilot runs closed with every step completed or excepted | At least one real run closes |
| Independent return | Pilot returns on a later day and updates the run without prompting or screen-sharing | At least one of two pilots |
| History utility | Pilot can identify a missed step and explain its outcome using product history | At least one observed successful task |
| Price intent | Pilot accepts a concrete $29/month offer or provides a specific rejection | Measured separately after product use; no pre-existing baseline |

Cold acquisition remains baselined at 27/300, or 9%. Warm acquisition remains 12/40, or 30%, and is never combined with cold traffic in reporting.

### 9.2 Continue, narrow, and kill triggers

Continue beyond six weeks if both pilots activate, at least one real run closes, and at least one pilot independently returns.

Narrow or revise the workflow if pilots activate but either:

- routinely leave owners or due dates meaningless,
- maintain a parallel spreadsheet for included P0 data,
- cannot interpret missed-step history, or
- require concurrent active runs before completing a first run.

Stop further feature development and return to discovery if neither volunteer starts a real run by the end of week 5 after receiving working access and one normal onboarding session.

Do not declare pricing validated unless at least one user faces a real $29/month decision. A verbal “sounds reasonable” is not sufficient. If both pilots reject the offer, document whether the cause is price, missing value, or purchasing timing before changing price.

## 10. Risks and open decisions

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| One-active-run limit feels artificial | Pilot may revert to another tool | Treat as an explicit validation boundary; do not build concurrency until observed |
| Owner labels do not notify people | Responsibility may remain social rather than operational | Make manual reminders and owner visibility clear; test whether accounts are actually needed |
| Manual missed-state processing is unreliable | History becomes untrustworthy | Calculate overdue state deterministically and test date/timezone transitions |
| Free-text fields receive sensitive data | Expands privacy burden | Use boundary copy and minimal fields; do not claim compliance |
| CSV expectations expand into migration | Consumes the schedule | Export only; keep import in non-goals |
| Pilot support becomes founder-operated workflow management | Produces false activation | Record when the developer enters or repairs customer data |

Founder decisions to confirm before implementation:

1. Whether “one active run” is a hard workspace limit for the pilot.
2. Whether an owner is strictly a label or must be selected from a small workspace directory.
3. The retention and deletion behavior promised to pilot users.

## 11. Six-week delivery plan

| Week | Milestone | Outcome and exit criteria |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | M0: Product skeleton | Authentication, workspace isolation, timezone choice, core entities, and state-transition tests exist; no file or bookkeeping models are introduced. |
| 2 | M1: First vertical slice | A user creates a three-step workflow, starts a run, sees owner/due state, resolves steps, and closes the run through persisted UI. |
| 3 | M2: Accountability history | Exceptions, overdue detection, immutable missed events, manual reminders, and chronological history pass transition tests. |
| 4 | M3: Boundaries and portability | External links and safe CSV export work; empty/error states, authorization checks, keyboard flow, and prohibited-capability review pass. |
| 5 | M4: Pilot-ready | Both volunteers are onboarded separately; each attempts a real run; founder assistance and usability failures are logged. |
| 6 | M5: Validation decision | Blocking defects are fixed; activation, return use, completed-run, and history-utility signals are assessed; a concrete $29/month offer is tested only after use. |

### First vertical slice

Build this before reminder, CSV, or polish work:

1. Authenticate one operator into one workspace.
2. Create a reusable workflow containing three ordered steps with owner labels and due offsets.
3. Start one client run from that workflow.
4. Display resolved due dates and current due states.
5. Complete one step and except another with a reason.
6. Preserve those events in step history.
7. Close the run only after all three steps are resolved.

The slice is complete when it can be demonstrated after a fresh sign-in using persisted data and without database or developer intervention.

## 12. Launch and feedback protocol

The first release is a private pilot for the two volunteers, not a public launch.

- Observe each pilot’s first workflow setup without taking control.
- Record product defects separately from requests and training confusion.
- Mark any founder-entered data so assisted activation is not counted as independent use.
- Conduct a short debrief after the first real run: what happened, what remained elsewhere, what was missed, and what they would do next time.
- Present the $29/month decision only after meaningful use and record the actual response.
- Do not infer retention from signup, pilot volunteering, or a single session.
