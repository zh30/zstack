### Hypothesis

If hands-on support agents at small US SaaS teams see a credible way to restore client-specific tab workspaces in one click, at least two cold sources will independently clear their pre-registered conversion thresholds during the 10-day test.

This test can establish waitlist interest. It cannot establish product usability, retention, technical feasibility, or willingness to pay.

### Evidence ladder

1. Qualified landing-page view
2. CTA click
3. Form attempt
4. Buttondown-accepted subscriber
5. Welcome-email reply describing an existing workflow or recent incident
6. Explicit reaction to planned $9/month pricing
7. Payment or invoice request

Only level 4 counts as the primary conversion. Levels 5–7 are stronger qualitative signals.

### Qualified visitor

Count a visitor only when:

- Plausible identifies a unique US visit with a recognized UTM source.
- The visit is not internal, a bot, a test submission, or an obvious accidental bounce.
- The referring channel was targeted at SaaS support practitioners or small SaaS operators.

Role qualification from the optional form answer is reported separately; it must not be retroactively used to manipulate the landing denominator.

## 2. Source-specific thresholds

Do not calculate a combined warm-and-cold conversion rate. Keep each source’s pageviews, subscribers, cost, and replies separate.

Rates below 50 qualified cold visits are directional and cannot trigger a product-level kill decision.

| Source | Minimum decision sample | Strong | Promising | Weak |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Reddit organic | 50 visits | ≥8% and ≥4 confirmed | 4–7.99% and ≥2 | <3% after 50 |
| X organic | 50 visits | ≥6% and ≥3 confirmed | 3–5.99% and ≥2 | <2% after 50 |
| Approved Slack/Discord communities | 50 visits | ≥10% and ≥5 confirmed | 5–9.99% and ≥3 | <3% after 50 |
| Reddit paid | 50 visits | ≥5%, ≥3 confirmed, CPL ≤$30 | 2–4.99% or CPL $30.01–$50 | <2% after 50, or $60 spent with zero confirmed |
| Warm direct outreach | 20 landing visits | ≥20%, ≥4 confirmed, and ≥3 substantive replies | 10–19.99% and ≥2 confirmed | <10% after 20 |

The warm result can refine language and generate interviews, but it cannot rescue weak cold acquisition.

For the paid source, pause if average CPC remains above $3 after at least $45 spend: at that price, the $150 cap cannot generate the 50-visit minimum. That is a channel-economics failure, not a product verdict.

### Problem

## A client replies. Your context has disappeared.

- You reopen the ticket, account, logs, docs, dashboard, and past conversations one tab at a time.

### Solution

## Pick the client. Get the workspace back.

Client Tabs is planned around one job:

1. **Save a workspace**  
   Group the tabs you regularly use for a client.

2. **Name the client context**  
   Keep each workspace identifiable without relying on browser history.

3. **Restore it in one click**  
   After setup, choose the client and reopen the saved tab set together.

### FAQ

#### Is Client Tabs available now?

No. It is an unbuilt Chrome extension concept being tested before development begins.

#### What does “one-click restore” mean?

After a client workspace has been configured, the intended workflow is to choose that client and reopen its saved tabs together with one action. The technical behavior still needs to be prototyped and tested.

#### When will it ship?

There is no firm ship date. Waitlist evidence will determine whether the project moves to a thin prototype.

#### How much will it cost?

The current plan is for paid pricing to start at $9/month. Final packaging may change as the product is validated. Joining the waitlist is free and creates no payment obligation.

#### What browser data will it access?

That has not been finalized because the extension is unbuilt. The intended design will request only the permissions needed to save and restore workspaces. Permission scope, storage choices, and handling of sensitive client URLs must be documented and tested before release.

#### What happens to my email?

It is stored by Buttondown and used only for early-access updates and product research. It will not be sold. Every message will include an unsubscribe option.

## 5. Implementation pack

### Architecture

```text
Cloudflare Pages
├── index.html
├── og-client-tabs.png
└── functions/api/waitlist
        ├── validates input and consent
        ├── maps allow-listed UTM values to a source class
        ├── submits subscriber + attribution to Buttondown
        └── returns confirmed success only after Buttondown accepts it

Plausible
├── pageview and UTM denominator
└── client events

Buttondown
├── subscriber source of truth
├── welcome email
├── source metadata/tag
└── reply and export records
```

Required placeholders:

- `YOUR_DOMAIN`
- `BUTTONDOWN_API_TOKEN` as a Cloudflare server-side secret
- `BUTTONDOWN_NEWSLETTER_ID`, if required by the current API
- Plausible site-domain configuration
- Contact email
- Current Buttondown API endpoint and supported metadata/tag field names

### Form fields

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `email` | Required; normalized and validated server-side |
| `pain` | Optional; maximum 500 characters |
| `consent` | Required, unchecked by default |
| `utm_source` | Hidden, allow-listed server-side |
| `utm_medium` | Hidden, allow-listed server-side |
| `utm_campaign` | Hidden; must equal the test campaign |
| `utm_content` | Hidden |
| `source_class` | Derived by the server, never trusted from the browser |
| `headline_variant` | Fixed to `a` during the test |
| `cta_location` | `nav`, `hero`, `offer`, or `final` |
| `website` | Hidden honeypot; valid submissions leave it blank |

Reject malformed emails, missing consent, unexpected campaign values, honeypot completion, oversized inputs, and rapid repeated requests.

### Attribution gate

Before launch, confirm that a real accepted Buttondown subscriber retains one of these exact tags or metadata values:

- `cold_reddit_organic`
- `cold_x_organic`
- `cold_community`
- `cold_reddit_paid`
- `warm_direct`

If Buttondown cannot preserve source metadata, create distinct provider-native forms/lists/tags for each source. Do not run the source-threshold experiment with one undifferentiated subscriber count.

### Submission states

- **Idle:** normal form.
- **Submitting:** disable the button and show `Sending…`.
- **Provider-confirmed success:** only after the Pages Function receives and validates a successful Buttondown response:

  > You’re on the early-access waitlist. Check your inbox for a note from Client Tabs.

- **Rejected or unavailable:**

  > We couldn’t add you to the list. Please check the email address and try again.

Never show success based on a timeout, query parameter, URL fragment, cookie, or browser storage.

### Plausible events

| Event | Trigger | Properties | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard pageview | Page loads | UTM values, variant `a` | Landing denominator |
| `cta_click` | Exact CTA clicked | `cta_location`, `source_class`, `variant` | Interest only |
| `waitlist_attempt` | Valid client form sent | `source_class`, `cta_location` | Attempt only |
| `waitlist_error` | Server/provider rejects request | `source_class`, `error_class` | Funnel diagnostic |
| `waitlist_confirmed` | Server has verified Buttondown accepted the subscriber | `source_class`, `cta_location`, `variant` | Analytics mirror of conversion |

Buttondown’s accepted subscriber export is the conversion source of truth. Plausible’s `waitlist_confirmed` event is a diagnostic mirror and can be blocked by browsers.

If double opt-in is required, rename the immediate event `waitlist_accepted_pending_confirmation`. Do not fire `waitlist_confirmed` until a verified provider webhook or export shows active confirmation.

### Preflight checklist

Run these before any promotion:

1. Test all four CTA placements.
2. Submit one valid email for every source class.
3. Verify the source tag on each Buttondown record.
4. Verify the welcome email and unsubscribe link.
5. Submit an invalid email and confirm it is not counted.
6. Trigger the honeypot and rate limit.
7. Confirm Plausible receives pageview, click, attempt, error, and confirmed events appropriately.
8. Test mobile layout and keyboard navigation.
9. Confirm no firm date, customer proof, or fake interface appears.
10. Exclude internal traffic from the dashboard.

## 10. Decision rules

### Proceed to a thin prototype

Proceed only when:

- Two cold sources independently meet their **Strong** thresholds with their own minimum samples; and
- At least three confirmed cold-source subscribers reply with a concrete recent workflow or incident; and
- At least two cold-source replies say $9/month is plausibly worthwhile.

The first prototype should test actual Chrome permission scope and one-click restoration—not a complete commercial extension.

An unsolicited credible payment or invoice request triggers a separate paid-intent test. One request is a lead, not proof of scale.

### Interview before building

Run interviews when:

- A cold source converts strongly but replies are silent or generic.
- Warm outreach is strong while every cold source is inconclusive.
- People join but repeatedly describe different problems.
- Price reactions are negative despite strong workflow pain.

### Retest the offer

Run a second controlled smoke test when a cold source reaches 50 visits and:

- CTA click rate is below 8%: test headline B against A.
- CTA click rate is at least 12% but confirmed CVR is weak: investigate trust, form friction, privacy, or price framing.
- Comments reveal that “workspace,” “client context,” or “one click” is misunderstood.

Change one variable at a time. Do not change the headline, audience, creative, and price simultaneously.

### Reshape or park

Reshape the problem or ICP if two cold sources each reach 50 qualified visits and both meet their **Weak** definitions.

Park the idea if a controlled retest also produces weak results and fewer than two substantive cold-source replies describe an urgent recurring problem.

Do not kill the product thesis when:

- No cold source reaches 50 qualified visits.
- Attribution fails.
- The ad platform cannot supply enough traffic within the cap.
- The form or welcome email malfunctions.

Those are distribution or experiment failures.

### Warm/cold rule

Warm outreach receives its own report and decision note. Its visitors, conversions, replies, and rates are never added to cold figures. Strong warm evidence may justify customer interviews, but not an MVP by itself.

## 11. Explicit non-build scope

During these 10 days, do not build:

- The Chrome extension or tab-restoration engine
- Authentication, accounts, billing, or team administration
- Cloud sync or client-data storage
- AI tab classification
- Help-desk, CRM, or Slack integrations
- A dashboard or full marketing site
- Fake UI, testimonials, logos, counters, or launch dates
- Multiple plans, discounts, referrals, or secondary CTAs

Build only the landing page, verified waitlist bridge, welcome email, attribution, and measurement.
