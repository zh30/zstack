# 10-Day Smoke-Test Package: ClientTab Restore

This package tests demand for an unbuilt Chrome extension for hands-on support agents at small US SaaS companies. It promises a planned one-click context restore without implying the product already exists.

Evidence limitation: web access is unavailable for this run. Current Buttondown API details, Plausible event syntax, Cloudflare runtime behavior, Reddit targeting options, and community rules could not be verified against official sources. The verification items below are launch blockers; do not buy traffic until they pass.

## 1. Smoke protocol

### Offer

- Working name: **ClientTab Restore**
- ICP: Hands-on customer support agents and support leads at US SaaS teams with roughly 2–25 support agents.
- Pain: Agents repeatedly find and reopen the same admin, CRM, ticket, billing, logs, and client-product tabs when switching customers.
- Planned outcome: Save a client-specific browser workspace and restore its tabs with one click.
- Stage: Concept validation; no extension has been built.
- Price signal: **Planned from $9/month**
- CTA everywhere on the page: **Join the early-access waitlist**
- Test period: **August 3–12, 2026**
- Paid-media ceiling: **$150 total**

### Hypothesis

If hands-on support agents at small US SaaS companies are offered one-click restoration of client-specific tab workspaces at a planned price from $9/month, qualified visitors will confirm their email for early access and some will reply with a current tab-switching workaround or willingness to pay.

### Funnel and evidence boundaries

```text
Qualified unique landing visitor
→ CTA click
→ form attempt
→ Buttondown accepts subscription request
→ visitor confirms double opt-in
→ confirmed waitlist member
→ welcome-email reply describing current behavior or purchase intent
```

The primary conversion is a **confirmed double-opt-in subscriber**, not a CTA click, form attempt, or merely accepted subscription request.

### Metrics

- Primary: confirmed waitlist members ÷ qualified unique visitors, calculated separately for every registered source.
- Secondary:
  - CTA clicks ÷ qualified visitors
  - Accepted subscription requests
  - Double-opt-in completion rate
  - Qualified replies ÷ confirmed members
  - Explicit price-positive replies
  - Cost per confirmed member for paid traffic
- Diagnostic only:
  - Email opens
  - Scroll depth
  - Raw traffic volume

A qualified reply states at least one of:

- A recent client-switching incident
- The tools/tabs currently reopened
- Frequency of the problem
- An existing workaround
- A credible reaction to paying from $9/month

### Sample discipline

Do not pool sources into a headline conversion rate. In particular:

- Warm one-to-one outreach remains its own lane.
- Cold paid traffic remains its own lane.
- Organic community, organic Reddit, and X each remain separate.
- Unknown or malformed attribution becomes `unattributed` and is excluded from source decisions.
- Rates below 50 qualified visitors are directional only.
- A source that cannot deliver its minimum sample indicates a distribution problem, not automatically a product rejection.

## 2. Pre-registered source thresholds

| Source | Minimum qualified sample | Continue signal | Reshape signal | Stop-source signal |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Warm one-to-one outreach | 30 visits | ≥6 confirmed, ≥3 qualified replies, and ≥2 price-positive replies | 3–5 confirmed or fewer than 3 qualified replies | ≤2 confirmed after 30 visits |
| Permission-based support community | 50 visits | ≥5 confirmed and ≥2 qualified replies | 2–4 confirmed | ≤1 confirmed |
| Organic Reddit | 75 visits | ≥5 confirmed and ≥2 qualified replies | 2–4 confirmed | ≤1 confirmed |
| X organic | 50 visits | ≥3 confirmed and ≥1 qualified reply | 1–2 confirmed | 0 confirmed |
| Cold Reddit ads | 100 visits | ≥5 confirmed, ≥2 qualified replies, and cost per confirmed member ≤$30 | 2–4 confirmed or cost per confirmed member $31–$60 | ≤1 confirmed, or zero accepted requests after 50 qualified visits |

These are experiment thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

A source may hit its conversion bar and still fail the demand test if confirmed members will not describe a real problem or engage with the price. Conversely, one credible “invoice me” reply is worth immediate follow-up, but is not proof of scalable demand.

## 6. Implementation package

### Architecture

```text
Cloudflare Pages
├── Static single-page landing page
├── /privacy
├── /api/waitlist — Cloudflare Pages Function
└── /og/clienttab-restore.png

Browser
├── Sanitizes and allow-lists attribution
├── Loads Plausible after query stripping
└── Sends form to same-origin /api/waitlist

Cloudflare Pages Function
├── Rate limits and validates email
├── Independently derives source from an opaque placement token
├── Sends subscription request to Buttondown
└── Returns only a bounded acceptance state

Buttondown
├── Stores subscriber and registered source metadata/tag
├── Runs double opt-in
└── Sends welcome email after confirmation
```

Do not expose the Buttondown API key in browser JavaScript.

### Required placeholders

- `YOUR_DOMAIN`
- `PLAUSIBLE_DOMAIN`
- `BUTTONDOWN_API_KEY`
- `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_SUBSCRIBER_ENDPOINT`
- `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_AUTH_HEADER`
- `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_ACCEPTED_STATUS`
- `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_EMAIL_FIELD`
- `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_METADATA_OR_TAG_FIELD`
- `VERIFY_BUTTONDOWN_DOUBLE_OPT_IN_STATE`
- `VERIFY_PLAUSIBLE_SCRIPT_URL`
- `VERIFY_PLAUSIBLE_CUSTOM_EVENT_SYNTAX`

Because official documentation could not be accessed, these mappings are unverified. Record the official documentation URL and verification date beside every resolved placeholder.

### Form contract

Visible field:

- `email`: required, trimmed, valid email format, maximum 254 characters

Server-generated fields:

- `request_id`: random non-PII identifier
- `placement_id`: derived from allow-listed opaque token
- `decision_lane`: derived server-side
- `variant`: fixed as `hero_a`
- `campaign`: fixed as `client_context_smoke_2026_08`
- `created_at`: server timestamp

Honeypot:

- `company_website`: visually hidden; reject if filled

The browser may report a placement token, but the server must independently look it up. It must never trust a client-supplied `decision_lane`, UTM, or source label.

### Server behavior

1. Accept only `POST` with JSON or form encoding chosen during implementation.
2. Enforce origin, request size, basic rate limits, honeypot, and email validation.
3. Resolve `placement_token` through an exact server-side allow-list.
4. Convert unknown, absent, expired, or malformed tokens to `unattributed`.
5. Submit the email and bounded attribution to Buttondown.
6. Treat only the verified documented Buttondown success response as `accepted`.
7. Never log email, raw request bodies, arbitrary UTMs, authorization headers, free text, or provider response bodies.
8. Log only `request_id`, bounded error category, HTTP status, placement ID, and decision lane.
9. Return one of:
   - `accepted`
   - `invalid_email`
   - `rate_limited`
   - `provider_rejected`
   - `provider_unavailable`
10. Never return Buttondown’s raw response to the browser.

### Double opt-in choice

Use double opt-in because this is a small B2B validation list where attribution quality and genuine intent matter more than maximizing raw submissions.

Buttondown’s confirmed active-subscriber record is the source of truth. An API-accepted request is a funnel step, not the primary conversion.

### Attribution reconciliation

Before launch, prove with one valid test for every registered placement that:

- The pageview appears under the correct bounded Plausible placement.
- The Buttondown request is accepted.
- The subscriber receives a confirmation message.
- The subscriber confirms.
- The confirmed record retains the same placement and lane.
- An invalid email does not create a conversion.
- An unknown token becomes `unattributed`.
- No raw UTM, email, or form value reaches Plausible.

If Buttondown cannot retain bounded attribution, create separate provider-native lists, forms, or verified tags for each source. If neither works, source-specific decisions are blocked.

## 13. Decision rules

### Build a thin MVP

Proceed only if at least one cold or permission-based organic source reaches its continue threshold and:

- There are at least five cold/organic confirmed members overall,
- At least three qualified replies describe a recent recurring workaround,
- At least two respondents react positively to the from-$9/month price, and
- Evidence comes from more than warm personal relationships.

The first MVP should do only: name a workspace, capture its tab URLs, list saved workspaces, and attempt one-click reopening. Permission design and sensitive URL handling must be resolved before testing.

### Interview before building

Interview confirmed members first if:

- Conversion is acceptable but qualified replies are scarce;
- Warm outreach succeeds while every cold/organic source fails;
- People describe the pain but object to Chrome permissions;
- People want session/login restoration that a tab URL cannot truthfully provide; or
- Interest clusters around a different job, such as incident workspaces rather than client workspaces.

### Rewrite and rerun

Test Headline B for another controlled traffic window if:

- Cold traffic delivers at least 100 qualified visitors,
- CTA click rate is under 3%,
- Confirmed conversion is under 2%, and
- Traffic-quality checks show the intended ICP is arriving.

Keep the product concept and source constant while changing the hero. Do not change the audience, price, page, and ad simultaneously.

### Narrow the ICP

Narrow to the recurring subgroup if replies consistently identify one environment—for example, support leads managing high-value accounts or agents investigating technical incidents—and that subgroup supplies at least three independent recent examples.

### Park the concept

Park or materially reshape it if:

- Two cold/organic sources each reach their minimum sample and stop-source threshold;
- There are fewer than three qualified replies across all confirmed cold/organic members;
- The dominant response is that existing tab groups or bookmarks already solve the job;
- The desired restore behavior depends on inaccessible login state or sensitive data handling that users will not trust; or
- The test cannot acquire qualified traffic within the timebox and no credible distribution wedge emerges.

Warm success alone cannot overrule cold failure.
