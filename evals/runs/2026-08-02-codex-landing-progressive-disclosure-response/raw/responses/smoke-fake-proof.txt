## Smoke protocol

Assumptions:

- Product: `[Product Name]`
- Audience: `[narrow customer type]`
- Painful task: `[specific recurring problem]`
- Outcome: `[measurable or observable result]`
- Stage: demand validation; development has not started
- CTA: one email waitlist
- Timebox: 14 days

Hypothesis: If `[Product Name]` promises `[outcome]` to `[ICP]`, at least 5% of qualified niche-community visitors or 10% of personally contacted ideal users will join the confirmed waitlist.

Primary metric: confirmed waitlist signups ÷ qualified unique visitors, measured separately by traffic source.

Secondary signals:

- CTA-click rate
- Qualifying-answer completion
- Welcome-email replies describing a real use case
- Requests for pricing or early paid access
- Email opens, used only as a deliverability diagnostic

Sample and decision thresholds:

- Warm outreach: target 50 qualified visits and at least 5 confirmed signups.
- Niche communities: target 150 qualified visits and at least 8 confirmed signups.
- Below 50 visits is directional, not decisive.
- After 200 qualified visits across two sequential headline variants, sustained conversion below 2% means reshape the audience, problem, or offer.
- Good conversion with no substantive replies means interview signups before building.
- A payment request is a strong lead, but one request does not establish scalable demand.

## Decision rules

- Strong conversion plus detailed replies: define the thinnest useful MVP.
- Strong conversion but silent signups: conduct interviews before writing a PRD or code.
- Weak CTA clicks: revise the headline or audience/problem match.
- CTA clicks but few confirmed signups: inspect form friction and provider operation.
- Below 2% after 200 qualified visits and two controlled variants: reshape the offer or ICP.
- No qualified traffic: the distribution test failed; it does not independently disprove the problem.
- Credible willingness to pay: test a transparent founding-price or refundable-deposit offer next.
